# Artefacts by stage

Part of [[Product Design Prompt Kit]].

Designers follow the Double Diamond: explore the problem widely, converge on the real one, explore solutions widely, converge on one. Each stage produces named artefacts. Asking for an artefact by name is the quickest way to sound like a designer.

## Discover

Understand the problem.

| Artefact | What it looks like | Question it answers | Ask for it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem brief** | A one-page written document. | What problem, for whom, and how will we know it worked? | Before any screens exist. |
| **Proto-personas and job stories** | Short profile cards, one per type of user. | Who exactly are we designing for, and in what situation? | You have a rough audience but no research yet. |
| **Interview guide** | A question script with timings. | What should we ask real users so we learn rather than confirm? | Before building, to test the brief. |
| **Competitive UX audit** | A comparison table, usually with screenshots. | What patterns do users already expect, and where is the gap? | Early, to avoid reinventing solved patterns. |

## Define

Choose what to solve.

| Artefact | What it looks like | Question it answers | Ask for it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Journey map** | A wide table: stages across the top, feelings and pain points down the side. | Where in the whole experience does it hurt most? | You need to choose which moment to design for. |
| **Requirements and user stories** | A prioritised list of one-line stories, each with pass-or-fail tests. | What exactly is in v1, and how will we know each piece is done? | The problem is agreed and you are scoping. |
| **Information architecture** | A tree diagram of screens, like an organisation chart. | How is everything organised and named, and how do people move around? | Before designing navigation or adding a new area. |
| **User flow** | A boxes-and-arrows flowchart. | What are all the steps and branches to get the job done? | Before wireframes. Flows come before screens. |

## Develop

Design the solution.

| Artefact | What it looks like | Question it answers | Ask for it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wireframes** | Grey box sketches of each screen, with no colour or styling. | What goes on each screen, in what priority? | The flow is agreed and you want layouts that are cheap to change. |
| **High-fidelity mockup** | Pictures of finished screens, accurate to the pixel. | What does the finished screen look and feel like? | Wireframes are approved. |
| **Clickable prototype** | Linked screens you can tap through as if the app existed. | Does the flow feel right when you actually tap through it? | Before usability testing or showing stakeholders. |
| **Design system and tokens** | A reference of colours, type, spacing and components. | What are the reusable rules so every screen stays consistent? | You have two or more screens, or before building many. |
| **Component spec** | A sheet showing one component in every variant and state. | Exactly how does one component look and behave in every case? | Handing a component to engineering, or standardising it. |
| **Interaction and motion spec** | A table of triggers and responses, plus short demos. | What happens when the user acts, and how does it feel? | Screens exist but feel static or unclear. |
| **UX writing and microcopy** | A spreadsheet of every string in the interface. | What exactly do the words say? | Any time. Words are half the interface. |

## Deliver

Check it and hand it over.

| Artefact | What it looks like | Question it answers | Ask for it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Heuristic review** | A list of problems ranked by severity. | What is wrong with this design, and how bad is it? | After every significant AI output, or on your current app. |
| **Accessibility audit** | A list of problems, each tied to a WCAG rule. | Can everyone use it, including with a screen reader or keyboard? | Before hand-off and before launch. |
| **Responsive and platform adaptation** | The same screen drawn at three widths. | How does this work on every screen size and platform? | A design exists for one size only. |
| **Usability test plan** | A script of tasks to give participants, and a note sheet. | Does it actually work for real people? | You have a clickable prototype. |
| **Developer handoff spec** | Annotated screens with measurements and behaviour notes. | Can engineering build it without guessing? | The design is final. |
