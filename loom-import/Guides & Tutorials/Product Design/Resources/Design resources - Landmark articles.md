# Design resources - Landmark articles

Part of [[11 Resource library]] in the [[Product Design Prompt Kit]].

The essays and studies designers quote to each other. Most take ten minutes and change how you look at a screen.

38 links. A star marks an essential. Last link check: 18 November 2026.

- **[Dieter Rams: ten principles for good design](https://www.vitsoe.com/gb/about/good-design)** ★ (Dieter Rams · Vitsœ; article; free). The ten principles behind Braun's products, which later shaped Apple's. The last one, 'as little design as possible', is the one designers quote most. *In a prompt:* "Apply 'good design is as little design as possible': what can be removed from this screen?"
- **[The $300 Million Button](https://articles.centercentre.com/three_hund_million_button/)** ★ (Jared Spool; article; free). How removing a forced registration step from a checkout added $300 million a year. The classic proof that one small flow decision can outweigh a redesign. *In a prompt:* "Where does this flow force a decision before the user has what they came for?"
- **[Never Use a Warning When you Mean Undo](https://alistapart.com/article/neveruseawarning/)** ★ (Aza Raskin · A List Apart; article; free). Why people click through warnings without reading them, and why undo is the respectful alternative. The source of 'undo over confirm' in this kit. *In a prompt:* "Replace every confirmation dialog on a reversible action with undo."
- **[You Are Not the User: The False-Consensus Effect](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/false-consensus/)** ★ (NN/g; article; free). Why your own preferences are poor evidence of what users want. The most important idea for anyone designing a product they also use. *In a prompt:* "List the assumptions here that rest only on my own habits, and how to test each."
- **[AI: First New UI Paradigm in 60 Years](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-paradigm/)** ★ (Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). Argues that telling a computer what outcome you want, rather than what steps to take, is a new kind of interface. It explains why prompting is a design skill. *In a prompt:* "Describe the outcome I want and let me check it, instead of asking me for each step."
- **[The Articulation Barrier](https://www.uxtigers.com/post/ai-articulation-barrier)** ★ (Jakob Nielsen · UX Tigers; article; free). Why describing what you want in prose is hard for most people, and how that limits AI tools. It names the problem this whole kit exists to solve. *In a prompt:* "Offer me choices to pick from wherever I would otherwise have to describe something in words."
- **[Designing accessible color systems](https://stripe.com/blog/accessible-color-systems)** ★ (Daryl Koopersmith & Wilson Miner · Stripe; article; free). How Stripe built a palette where every colour at the same step has the same contrast. The model for any modern colour system. *In a prompt:* "Build the palette so every hue at the same step has the same contrast against white."
- **[First Principles of Interaction Design](https://asktog.com/atc/principles-of-interaction-design/)** (Bruce Tognazzini; article; free). A long, authoritative list of principles from the founder of Apple's Human Interface Group: anticipation, autonomy, defaults, Fitts's law, latency reduction and more. *In a prompt:* "Check this design against Tog's principles of anticipation and sensible defaults."
- **[The Eight Golden Rules of Interface Design](https://www.cs.umd.edu/users/ben/goldenrules.html)** (Ben Shneiderman; article; free). Eight rules from 1986 that still hold: consistency, shortcuts, feedback, closure, error prevention, reversal, control and low memory load. *In a prompt:* "Which of Shneiderman's eight golden rules does this flow break?"
- **[How Users Read on the Web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/)** (Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). The 1997 study whose first line is the summary: they don't, they scan. The reason for short paragraphs, headings and bullet lists. *In a prompt:* "Rewrite this screen's text to be scannable: highlighted keywords, short lists, one idea per paragraph."
- **[F-Shaped Pattern of Reading on the Web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)** (NN/g; article; free). Eye-tracking evidence of how people skim pages, and how to lay out content so the important parts get seen. *In a prompt:* "Front-load the first two words of every heading and list item."
- **[The Aesthetic-Usability Effect](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/)** (NN/g; article; free). People judge attractive interfaces as easier to use and forgive their flaws. The business case for visual polish, and a warning about what it can hide. *In a prompt:* "Is the visual polish here hiding a usability problem that testing would expose?"
- **[Progressive Disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)** (Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). The original statement of the idea: show the few most important options first and offer the rest on request. *In a prompt:* "Split these settings into a primary set and an advanced set shown on request."
- **[Mental Models](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mental-models/)** (Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). What users believe about how a system works, why that belief drives their behaviour, and what to do when it is wrong. *In a prompt:* "What mental model will a new user bring to this, and where does the design contradict it?"
- **[Minimize Cognitive Load to Maximize Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/minimize-cognitive-load/)** (NN/g; article; free). A short guide to reducing the mental effort an interface demands: cut clutter, build on what people know, offload tasks. *In a prompt:* "Cut the cognitive load of this screen: what can be removed, defaulted or remembered for the user?"
- **[Memory Recognition and Recall in User Interfaces](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall/)** (NN/g; article; free). Why showing options beats asking people to remember them. The reasoning behind menus, recent items and autocomplete. *In a prompt:* "Let people recognise options here instead of recalling them from memory."
- **[Hamburger Menus and Hidden Navigation Hurt UX Metrics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)** (NN/g; article; free). The study showing that hidden navigation is used less and found more slowly. The evidence behind 'out of sight, out of mind'. *In a prompt:* "Keep the top four destinations visible; hide only the rest."
- **[Placeholders in Form Fields Are Harmful](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-placeholders/)** (NN/g; article; free). Why grey hint text inside a field is a poor substitute for a label, with seven specific problems it causes. *In a prompt:* "Put a visible label above every field; never use the placeholder as the label."
- **[The Definition of User Experience (UX)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/definition-user-experience/)** (Don Norman & Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). The original definition from the people who coined the term, and how UX differs from UI and usability. *In a prompt:* "Separate the UX problems here from the UI problems, and list each."
- **[Usability 101: Introduction to Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-101-introduction-to-usability/)** (Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). What usability is, its five components, and why it matters, in a few minutes. *In a prompt:* "Rate this flow on learnability, efficiency, memorability, errors and satisfaction."
- **[Design Thinking 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-thinking/)** (NN/g; article; free). The six-phase process in brief: empathise, define, ideate, prototype, test, implement. *In a prompt:* "Which design thinking phase are we in, and what would finishing it properly look like?"
- **[Mobile First](https://www.lukew.com/ff/933/mobile-first)** (Luke Wroblewski; article; free). The 2009 post that argued for designing the small screen first because its constraints force focus. *In a prompt:* "Design the 375 px version first. What survives when there is only room for one thing?"
- **[Obvious Always Wins](https://www.lukew.com/ff/1945/obvious-always-wins)** (Luke Wroblewski; article; free). Real product data showing that visible navigation beats hidden navigation for engagement. *In a prompt:* "Make the core actions visible at all times, even if it looks less minimal."
- **[A Dao of Web Design](https://alistapart.com/article/dao/)** (John Allsopp · A List Apart; article; free). The essay from 2000 that told designers to embrace the web's flexibility instead of fighting it. The root of responsive design. *In a prompt:* "Let the layout adapt to the reader's screen and settings instead of fixing every dimension."
- **[Magic Ink](https://worrydream.com/MagicInk/)** (Bret Victor; article; free). A long, influential argument that most software should show information well instead of demanding interaction. *In a prompt:* "What could this screen show automatically, from context, so the user doesn't have to ask?"
- **[A Brief Rant on the Future of Interaction Design](https://worrydream.com/ABriefRantOnTheFutureOfInteractionDesign/)** (Bret Victor; article; free). A short, sharp essay on how touchscreens ignore what hands can do. A reminder to question inherited conventions. *In a prompt:* "What does this interaction ignore about how people naturally hold and handle things?"
- **[The Web's Grain](https://frankchimero.com/blog/2015/the-webs-grain/)** (Frank Chimero; article; free). An illustrated essay on designing with the nature of the medium, edgeless and fluid, not against it. *In a prompt:* "Design this for a canvas with no fixed edges."
- **[What Screens Want](https://frankchimero.com/blog/2013/what-screens-want/)** (Frank Chimero; article; free). An essay on what is native to screens: change, movement and responsiveness, not imitation of physical things. *In a prompt:* "Where should this interface change over time instead of sitting still?"
- **[Taste for Makers](https://paulgraham.com/taste.html)** (Paul Graham; article; free). An essay on what good design has in common across fields: simple, timeless, solves the right problem, looks easy. *In a prompt:* "Does this solve the right problem, and does the result look easy?"
- **[The Laws of Simplicity](https://lawsofsimplicity.com/)** (John Maeda; reference; free). Ten laws for balancing simplicity and complexity, starting with thoughtful reduction. *In a prompt:* "Apply Maeda's first law: reduce thoughtfully. What can go without anyone missing it?"
- **[Calm Technology](https://calmtech.com/)** (Amber Case; reference; free). Principles for technology that informs without demanding attention. A natural fit for a quiet notes app. *In a prompt:* "Use the periphery: how can this inform the user without interrupting them?"
- **[8-Point Grid](https://spec.fm/specifics/8-pt-grid)** (Bryn Jackson · Spec; article; free). The short article that popularised sizing and spacing everything in multiples of eight. *In a prompt:* "Size and space every element in multiples of 8, with 4 for tight cases."
- **[Atomic Design (the original post)](https://bradfrost.com/blog/post/atomic-web-design/)** (Brad Frost; article; free). The 2013 blog post that introduced atoms, molecules and organisms, before it became a book. *In a prompt:* "Start from the smallest reusable parts and compose upwards."
- **[The Complete Guide to the Kano Model](https://foldingburritos.com/blog/kano-model/)** (Daniel Zacarias · Folding Burritos; article; free). How to sort features into must-haves, performance features and delighters, with the survey method and analysis. *In a prompt:* "Classify these features as must-be, performance or attractive using the Kano model."
- **[How Superhuman Built an Engine to Find Product Market Fit](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)** (Rahul Vohra · First Round Review; article; free). A measurable method for deciding what to build next, based on asking users how disappointed they would be without the product. *In a prompt:* "Write the 'how disappointed would you be' survey for Loom and say how to act on the results."
- **[How we redesigned the Linear UI](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)** (Linear; case studies; free). A team known for craft explains a full interface redesign: goals, constraints, colour system and rollout. *In a prompt:* "Plan this redesign in stages, with what stays fixed and what changes at each one."
- **[Connect: behind the front-end experience](https://stripe.com/blog/connect-front-end-experience)** (Benjamin De Cock · Stripe; case studies; free). A detailed look at the layout, animation and performance craft in one celebrated landing page. *In a prompt:* "Explain the craft details that would lift this page from correct to memorable."
- **[Style is consistent constraint](https://stephango.com/style)** (Steph Ango; article; free). A very short essay: style comes from choosing constraints and holding to them. *In a prompt:* "List the five constraints that define this product's style, and enforce them everywhere."
