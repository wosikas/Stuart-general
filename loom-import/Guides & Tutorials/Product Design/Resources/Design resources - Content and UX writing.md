# Design resources - Content and UX writing

Part of [[11 Resource library]] in the [[Product Design Prompt Kit]].

The words in the interface, which do as much work as the layout.

10 links. A star marks an essential. Last link check: 18 November 2026.

- **[Mailchimp Content Style Guide](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/)** ★ (Mailchimp; guideline; free). The model voice-and-tone guide. Shows how to stay one personality while adapting tone to the reader's mood. *In a prompt:* "Write in a voice that is plain and warm, never cute. Adapt tone to error versus success."
- **[Error-Message Guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/)** ★ (NN/g; article; free). How to write errors that are visible, precise and constructive, with a scoring rubric. *In a prompt:* "Each error says what happened and how to fix it, next to the field, with no blame."
- **[Designing Empty States in Complex Applications](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)** (NN/g; article; free). Three jobs an empty state can do: confirm status, teach, and offer a direct path to the first action. *In a prompt:* "Design the empty state to explain, teach and offer one starting action."
- **[Material 3: Content design](https://m3.material.io/foundations/content-design/overview)** (Google; guideline; free). Google's UX writing guidance: style, word choice, notifications and alt text. *In a prompt:* "Follow Material content design: sentence case, second person, no exclamation marks."
- **[Apple HIG: Writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing)** (Apple; guideline; free). Apple's guidance on clear, consistent interface text, including buttons and alerts. *In a prompt:* "Write alert titles as short questions or statements, with verb buttons."
- **[Writing to GOV.UK standards](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/)** (UK Government Digital Service; guideline; free). How to write for the way people actually read on screens: front-loaded, plain and scannable. *In a prompt:* "Rewrite this at a reading age of nine, front-loading the key words."
- **[Microsoft Writing Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/welcome/)** (Microsoft; guideline; free). A complete style guide with top-ten tips and word lists for interface text. *In a prompt:* "Apply Microsoft's top ten tips: bigger ideas, fewer words."
- **[Hemingway Editor](https://hemingwayapp.com/)** (Hemingway; tool; free tier). Highlights long sentences, passive voice and hard words. Paste interface text in to check its reading level. *In a prompt:* "Simplify all strings to grade 6 reading level."
- **[Strategic Writing for UX](https://www.torreypodmajersky.com/)** (Torrey Podmajersky; book; paid). The standard book on UX writing, with patterns for titles, buttons, errors and empty states. *In a prompt:* "Create a voice chart: concepts, vocabulary, verbosity, grammar and punctuation."
- **[UX Writing: Study Guide](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-writing-study-guide/)** (NN/g; reference; free). An index of NN/g's articles on writing for interfaces: tone, plain language, errors and microcopy. *In a prompt:* "Give me the rules for writing button labels, with examples."
