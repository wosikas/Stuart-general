# Design resources - Component and pattern guides

Part of [[11 Resource library]] in the [[Product Design Prompt Kit]].

How to design one specific thing well: forms, navigation, tables, dialogs, sheets and controls.

33 links. A star marks an essential. Last link check: 18 November 2026.

- **[Website Forms Usability: Top 10 Recommendations](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-form-design/)** ★ (NN/g; article; free). Ten rules that cover most form mistakes: keep it short, one column, labels above, group related fields, mark optional fields. *In a prompt:* "Apply NN/g's ten form recommendations and tell me which ones this form breaks."
- **[Design better data tables](https://mattstromawn.com/writing/tables/)** ★ (Matt Ström-Awn; article; free). The best short guide to tables: align numbers right, text left, use tabular figures, and remove most of the lines. *In a prompt:* "Right-align numbers, left-align text, use tabular figures, and remove vertical rules."
- **[Smart Interface Design Patterns](https://smart-interface-design-patterns.com/)** ★ (Vitaly Friedman; course; free tier). Evidence-based guidelines and checklists for dozens of specific patterns, from mega-menus to date pickers. The articles are free; the video course is paid. *In a prompt:* "Give me a design checklist for this pattern, with the edge cases people forget."
- **[Web Application Form Design](https://www.lukew.com/ff/1502/web-application-form-design)** (Luke Wroblewski; article; free). An early, influential guide to label alignment and form layout that still shapes how forms are designed. *In a prompt:* "Use top-aligned labels and a single clear path to completion."
- **[Inline Validation in Web Forms](https://alistapart.com/article/inline-validation-in-web-forms/)** (Luke Wroblewski · A List Apart; article; free). A usability study of when to validate: after the field, not during typing, and confirm success as well as failure. *In a prompt:* "Validate each field when the user leaves it, and confirm correct answers too."
- **[Sign Up Forms Must Die](https://alistapart.com/article/signupforms/)** (Luke Wroblewski · A List Apart; article; free). The case for letting people use a product before asking them to register: gradual engagement. *In a prompt:* "Let people capture their first note before asking them to create an account."
- **[Best Practices For Mobile Form Design](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2018/08/best-practices-for-mobile-form-design/)** (Nick Babich · Smashing Magazine; article; free). Mobile specifics: the right keyboard per field, input masks, autofill and thumb-friendly controls. *In a prompt:* "Set the correct keyboard type and autofill hint for every field."
- **[Better Form Design: One Thing Per Page](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2017/05/better-form-design-one-thing-per-page/)** (Adam Silver · Smashing Magazine; article; free). A case study of splitting a long form into single-question screens, and why it reduced errors. *In a prompt:* "Split this form so each screen asks for one thing."
- **[One thing per page](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2015/07/03/one-thing-per-page/)** (Tim Paul · GOV.UK; article; free). The short government post that named the pattern, with the reasoning behind it. *In a prompt:* "Use one question per screen and explain what that costs and gains here."
- **[GOV.UK: Structuring forms](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/form-structure)** (UK Government Digital Service; guideline; free). How to order questions, when to branch, and how to work out what to ask at all, using a question protocol. *In a prompt:* "Write a question protocol: for each field, why we need it and what we do with the answer."
- **[GOV.UK: Designing good questions](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/designing-good-questions)** (UK Government Digital Service; guideline; free). How to word questions and choose the right control so people can answer correctly first time. *In a prompt:* "Reword each question so it can be answered without rereading."
- **[GOV.UK Design System: Components](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/)** (UK Government Digital Service; guideline; free). Each component page says when to use it, when not to, and what user research found. *In a prompt:* "Follow the GOV.UK guidance on when not to use this component."
- **[Basic Patterns for Mobile Navigation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-navigation-patterns/)** (NN/g; article; free). A primer on tab bars, hamburger menus, navigation hubs and when each one fits. *In a prompt:* "Which mobile navigation pattern fits five top-level sections, and why not the others?"
- **[Apple HIG: Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)** (Apple; guideline; free). Apple's rules for bottom navigation: how many tabs, what belongs in them, and what never should. *In a prompt:* "Follow HIG tab bar rules: sections only, no actions, and no hiding the bar."
- **[Apple HIG: Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)** (Apple; guideline; free). When to use a sheet, how detents and the grabber work, and how sheets should be dismissed. The rulebook for the Quick Capture example. *In a prompt:* "Use a sheet with medium and large detents and a visible grabber, as HIG specifies."
- **[Material 3: Bottom sheets](https://m3.material.io/components/bottom-sheets/overview)** (Google; guideline; free). Standard versus modal bottom sheets on Android, with anatomy and measurements. *In a prompt:* "Use a Material 3 modal bottom sheet with a drag handle and scrim."
- **[Modal & Nonmodal Dialogs: When (& When Not) to Use Them](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog/)** (NN/g; article; free). When interrupting the user is justified and when it isn't, with alternatives. *In a prompt:* "Is a modal justified here, or would inline or nonmodal presentation serve better?"
- **[Dropdowns: Design Guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/drop-down-menus/)** (NN/g; article; free). When a dropdown helps, when it hurts, and what to use for very short or very long lists. *In a prompt:* "Replace dropdowns with fewer than five options with radio buttons."
- **[Checkboxes vs. Radio Buttons](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/checkboxes-vs-radio-buttons/)** (Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). The definitive rules for choosing between the two, and for labelling them. *In a prompt:* "Check every selection control: radio for one of many, checkbox for any of many."
- **[Toggle-Switch Guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/toggle-switch-guidelines/)** (NN/g; article; free). When a toggle is right, how to label it, and why it must take effect immediately. *In a prompt:* "Use toggles only for settings that apply instantly; label the setting, not the state."
- **[Tooltip Guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tooltip-guidelines/)** (NN/g; article; free). What belongs in a tooltip, what never should, and how to make them work without a mouse. *In a prompt:* "Move anything essential out of tooltips and into visible text."
- **[Cards: UI-Component Definition](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/cards-component/)** (NN/g; article; free). What a card is for, and when a plain list or table communicates better. *In a prompt:* "Would a list be easier to scan than these cards? Justify the choice."
- **[Touch Targets on Touchscreens](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/)** (NN/g; article; free). The research behind minimum target sizes and the spacing needed between them. *In a prompt:* "Make every target at least 1 cm square, with space between adjacent targets."
- **[Icon Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)** (NN/g; article; free). Why most icons are not understood without a label, and how to test the ones you use. *In a prompt:* "Add a text label to every icon that isn't universally recognised."
- **[Search: Visible and Simple](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/search-visible-and-simple/)** (Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). The basics of search design: an open box, where people expect it, with forgiving results. *In a prompt:* "Show search as an open field, not an icon, on every screen where finding matters."
- **[Progress Indicators Make a Slow System Less Insufferable](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/)** (NN/g; article; free). Which indicator to use for each length of wait, and why showing progress makes waits feel shorter. *In a prompt:* "Use a looping indicator under 10 seconds and a percent-done bar above it."
- **[Data Tables: Four Major User Tasks](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/data-tables/)** (NN/g; article; free). What people do with tables: find, compare, view or edit a row, and act on records. Design for each. *In a prompt:* "Design this table for finding a record, comparing rows and acting on several at once."
- **[Dashboards: Making Charts and Graphs Easier to Understand](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/)** (NN/g; article; free). How to use length, position and colour so a dashboard is understood at a glance. *In a prompt:* "Encode the key comparison with length or position, not area or colour alone."
- **[Mobile-App Onboarding: An Analysis of Components and Techniques](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-app-onboarding/)** (NN/g; article; free). What onboarding screens really achieve, and why most tutorials should be skipped or cut. *In a prompt:* "Cut the onboarding to what the user must know before first use, and teach the rest in context."
- **[Dark Mode vs. Light Mode: Which Is Better?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/)** (NN/g; article; free). What vision research says about each mode, and why offering the choice is the right answer. *In a prompt:* "Support both themes and follow the system setting by default."
- **[Material Design: Dark theme](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html)** (Google; guideline; free). The clearest technical guide to dark themes: dark grey not black, desaturated accents, elevation shown by lightness. *In a prompt:* "Use dark grey surfaces, lighter surfaces for higher elevation, and desaturated accents."
- **[True Lies Of Optimistic User Interfaces](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2016/11/true-lies-of-optimistic-user-interfaces/)** (Denys Mishunov · Smashing Magazine; article; free). How optimistic UI works, the psychology of why it feels fast, and when it is safe to use. *In a prompt:* "Use optimistic UI for saves, and specify what the user sees if the save later fails."
- **[GoodUI](https://goodui.org/)** (Jakub Linowski; reference; free tier). Interface patterns backed by published A/B test results, showing what moved the numbers. *In a prompt:* "Which tested patterns apply to this sign-up screen?"
