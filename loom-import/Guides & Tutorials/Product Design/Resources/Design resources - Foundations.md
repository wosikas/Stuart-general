# Design resources - Foundations

Part of [[11 Resource library]] in the [[Product Design Prompt Kit]].

Read these first. They hold the principles everything else rests on.

19 links. A star marks an essential. Last link check: 18 November 2026.

- **[Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/)** ★ (Jon Yablonski; reference; free). The psychology principles behind good interfaces, one clear page each. The quickest way to learn the names designers cite. *In a prompt:* "Which Laws of UX does this screen break? Name each one and fix it."
- **[10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)** ★ (Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). The ten rules every heuristic review is scored against, with examples. Read it before you ask an AI to critique anything. *In a prompt:* "Review this flow against Nielsen's 10 heuristics, with severity from 0 to 4."
- **[NN/g article library](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/)** (Nielsen Norman Group; reference; free). Thousands of research-backed articles. Search any pattern name here before you design it. *In a prompt:* "Follow NN/g guidance on bottom sheets and tell me where you deviate, and why."
- **[The Design of Everyday Things](https://jnd.org/books/)** (Don Norman; book; paid). The origin of affordance, signifier, feedback and mental model. Chapters 1 and 2 give you most of the vocabulary. *In a prompt:* "Where does this design lack a signifier, and where is feedback missing?"
- **[Don't Make Me Think](https://sensible.com/dont-make-me-think/)** ★ (Steve Krug; book; paid). Usability basics in a weekend read. It trains you to notice friction, which is what makes your feedback prompts sharp. *In a prompt:* "Apply Krug's first law: what on this screen makes a new user stop and think?"
- **[Refactoring UI](https://www.refactoringui.com/)** ★ (Adam Wathan & Steve Schoger; book; paid). Visual design tactics for people who aren't designers: hierarchy, spacing, colour, depth. The fastest route to judging UI quality. *In a prompt:* "De-emphasise the secondary content instead of emphasising the primary action further."
- **[7 Rules for Creating Gorgeous UI](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html)** ★ (Erik Kennedy; article; free). A free two-part crash course in visual design, written by an engineer who taught himself. Covers light, contrast, whitespace and text over images. *In a prompt:* "Light comes from the sky: check that every shadow and highlight is consistent."
- **[The Elements of User Experience](http://www.jjg.net/elements/pdf/elements.pdf)** (Jesse James Garrett; reference; free). The original one-page diagram of the five planes used in section 01 of this kit. *In a prompt:* "Which plane is this decision on, and have the planes beneath it been settled?"
- **[The Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/)** (Design Council; reference; free). The source of the Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver stages used throughout this kit. *In a prompt:* "We are in Define. Don't propose solutions yet; narrow the problem."
- **[Shape Up](https://basecamp.com/shapeup)** (Ryan Singer · Basecamp; book; free). How to shape work before design starts: appetite, breadboards and fat-marker sketches. Free to read online. *In a prompt:* "Breadboard this feature: places, affordances and connection lines, with no visuals."
- **[Checklist Design](https://www.checklist.design/)** (George Hatzis; reference; free). Checklists of what a login page, a modal, a table and dozens of other pages and components should contain. *In a prompt:* "Check this sign-up flow against a best-practice checklist and list what is missing."
- **[Design Principles](https://principles.design/)** (Ben Brignell; reference; free). An open collection of product design principles from many teams. Useful raw material for writing your own. *In a prompt:* "Draft five design principles for Loom, each naming the trade-off it settles."
- **[Government Design Principles](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/government-design-principles)** (UK Government Digital Service; guideline; free). Ten short principles, starting with user needs and doing less. A model of how to write principles that settle arguments. *In a prompt:* "Apply 'do the hard work to make it simple': what complexity can the system absorb?"
- **[Growth.design case studies](https://growth.design/case-studies)** ★ (Dan Benoni & Louis-Xavier Lavallée; case studies; free). Comic-style teardowns of real product flows, each tied to named psychology principles. Enjoyable and dense with vocabulary. *In a prompt:* "Tear down our onboarding in the style of a Growth.design case study."
- **[Built for Mars](https://builtformars.com/)** (Peter Ramsey; case studies; free). Long, careful UX case studies of well-known apps, counting clicks and naming each friction point. *In a prompt:* "Count the steps in this flow and benchmark it against the best in class."
- **[Deceptive Patterns](https://www.deceptive.design/)** (Harry Brignull; reference; free). A catalogue of manipulative interface tricks, with the laws they break. Know them so you can refuse them. *In a prompt:* "Check this cancellation flow for deceptive patterns and remove any you find."
- **[Humane by Design](https://humanebydesign.com/)** (Jon Yablonski; reference; free). Principles for products that respect attention and wellbeing. A good fit for a calm notes app. *In a prompt:* "Review this notification design against humane design principles."
- **[Interaction Design Foundation: topics](https://ixdf.org/literature/topics)** (IxDF; reference; free). Encyclopaedia-style explanations of almost every UX term, each with further reading. *In a prompt:* "Explain this concept the way IxDF would, then apply it to my screen."
- **[GOV.UK Service Manual](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual)** (UK Government Digital Service; guideline; free). How a mature team runs design from discovery to live service. Plain, practical and tested at national scale. *In a prompt:* "What would a GOV.UK service assessment ask about this design at alpha stage?"
