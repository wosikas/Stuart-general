# Design resources - Research and strategy

Part of [[11 Resource library]] in the [[Product Design Prompt Kit]].

How to find out what people need before any screen exists, how to test afterwards, and how to explain your decisions.

44 links. A star marks an essential. Last link check: 18 November 2026.

- **[The Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/)** ★ (Rob Fitzpatrick; book; paid). How to ask customers questions they can't answer politely and falsely. The best short book on interviewing. *In a prompt:* "Rewrite these interview questions so they pass the Mom Test."
- **[UX Research Cheat Sheet](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-research-cheat-sheet/)** ★ (NN/g; article; free). Which research method to use at each stage of a project, on one page. *In a prompt:* "Which research method fits where we are, and what would it cost us to skip it?"
- **[Why You Only Need to Test with 5 Users](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/)** (Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). The reasoning behind small, frequent usability tests. Explains why five people find most problems. *In a prompt:* "Plan three rounds of five-person tests, not one large study."
- **[Journey Mapping 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/journey-mapping-101/)** (NN/g; article; free). What a journey map contains, with a worked example and common variations. *In a prompt:* "Build a journey map with the lanes NN/g describes: actions, mindsets, emotions, opportunities."
- **[Card Sorting](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/card-sorting-definition/)** (NN/g; article; free). How to find out how users group and name things, the basis of good navigation. *In a prompt:* "Design an open card sort for our note types, with 30 cards."
- **[Know Your Customers' Jobs to Be Done](https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done)** (Clayton Christensen et al. · HBR; article; free tier). The article that made jobs-to-be-done mainstream, including the milkshake story. *In a prompt:* "What job is someone hiring Quick Capture to do, and what do they fire to do it?"
- **[Designing features using Job Stories](https://www.intercom.com/blog/using-job-stories-design-features-ui-ux/)** (Alan Klement · Intercom; article; free). The article that introduced the job story format used in this kit: when, I want to, so I can. *In a prompt:* "Write job stories, not user stories, for this feature."
- **[Measuring the User Experience on a Large Scale](https://research.google/pubs/measuring-the-user-experience-on-a-large-scale-user-centered-metrics-for-web-applications/)** (Kerry Rodden, Hilary Hutchinson & Xin Fu · Google; article; free). The original paper behind the HEART framework and the goals-signals-metrics process. *In a prompt:* "For each HEART category, give me a goal, a signal and a metric."
- **[Personas vs. Jobs-to-Be-Done](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/personas-jobs-be-done/)** (NN/g; article; free). When to describe who the user is, when to describe what they are trying to do, and why you usually want both. *In a prompt:* "Give me one persona and the three jobs they hire this product for."
- **[Product Talk](https://www.producttalk.org/)** (Teresa Torres; blog; free). Continuous discovery: weekly customer conversations and opportunity solution trees that link outcomes to ideas. *In a prompt:* "Draw an opportunity solution tree from this outcome down to testable ideas."
- **[The Design Sprint](https://www.gv.com/sprint/)** (GV; reference; free). A five-day process for going from a problem to a tested prototype. Useful as a template for compressing work. *In a prompt:* "Run a compressed design sprint on this problem: map, sketch, decide, prototype, test."
- **[IDEO Design Kit: Methods](https://www.designkit.org/methods.html)** (IDEO.org; reference; free). Step-by-step instructions for dozens of human-centred design methods. *In a prompt:* "Run the 'Five Whys' on this user complaint."
- **[UX Research Field Guide](https://www.userinterviews.com/ux-research-field-guide)** (User Interviews; reference; free). A free, well-organised guide to planning, recruiting, running and synthesising research. *In a prompt:* "Write a research plan with objectives, method, participants and timeline."
- **[GOV.UK: User research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research)** (UK Government Digital Service; guideline; free). Practical guides to every research activity, written for teams doing it for the first time. *In a prompt:* "Write a discussion guide the way the GOV.UK Service Manual recommends."
- **[Usability test script](https://sensible.com/download-files/)** (Steve Krug; kit; free). The free test script, checklist and consent form from Rocket Surgery Made Easy. Ready to use as written. *In a prompt:* "Adapt Krug's usability test script for our capture prototype."
- **[MeasuringU](https://measuringu.com/blogs/)** (Jeff Sauro & Jim Lewis; blog; free). The reference for UX measurement: the System Usability Scale, the Single Ease Question, sample sizes. *In a prompt:* "Add the System Usability Scale to the end of the test and explain how to score it."
- **[SVPG articles](https://www.svpg.com/articles/)** (Marty Cagan · Silicon Valley Product Group; blog; free). How strong product teams work: discovery versus delivery, the four product risks, empowered teams. *In a prompt:* "Assess this idea against the four risks: value, usability, feasibility, viability."
- **[Maze](https://maze.co/)** (Maze; tool; free tier). Run unmoderated tests on prototypes and get task success rates and click paths. *In a prompt:* "Write three Maze missions with success paths for this prototype."
- **[Lyssna](https://www.lyssna.com/)** (Lyssna; tool; free tier). Quick tests with real people: five-second tests, first-click tests, preference tests and surveys. *In a prompt:* "Design a five-second test for this home screen: what should people recall?"
- **[Optimal Workshop](https://www.optimalworkshop.com/)** (Optimal Workshop; tool; free tier). The standard tools for card sorting and tree testing, to validate navigation before you build it. *In a prompt:* "Write ten tree-test tasks to validate this sitemap."
- **[When to Use Which User-Experience Research Methods](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/which-ux-research-methods/)** ★ (Christian Rohrer · NN/g; article; free). The standard map of twenty research methods, placed on three axes. Tells you which method answers which kind of question. *In a prompt:* "Which research method answers this question: what people do, or what they say?"
- **[Articulating Design Decisions](https://tomgreever.com/articulating-design-decisions-book/)** ★ (Tom Greever; book; paid). How to explain and defend design choices to people who are not designers. Directly useful for asking an AI to justify its choices, and for judging the answer. *In a prompt:* "For each decision, say what problem it solves, how it affects the user, and why it beats the alternative."
- **[UX Mapping Methods Compared: A Cheat Sheet](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-mapping-cheat-sheet/)** (NN/g; article; free). Empathy maps, journey maps, experience maps and service blueprints side by side, with when to use each. *In a prompt:* "Which mapping method fits here: empathy map, journey map or service blueprint?"
- **[User Interviews 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-interviews/)** (NN/g; article; free). How to plan and run an interview, with the common mistakes. *In a prompt:* "Plan five user interviews: goals, who to recruit, and the opening questions."
- **[Usability Testing 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-101/)** (NN/g; article; free). The parts of a usability test, the roles involved and the main variations. *In a prompt:* "Design a 30-minute usability test of this prototype."
- **[Thinking Aloud: The #1 Usability Tool](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/thinking-aloud-the-1-usability-tool/)** (Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). The simplest and most valuable test method: ask people to say what they are thinking as they use the product. *In a prompt:* "Write the instructions I give a participant for a think-aloud test."
- **[Severity Ratings for Usability Problems](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-rate-the-severity-of-usability-problems/)** (Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). The 0 to 4 scale used in the heuristic review template in this kit, and how to apply it. *In a prompt:* "Rate each issue from 0 to 4 by frequency, impact and persistence."
- **[Empathy Mapping](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empathy-mapping/)** (NN/g; article; free). A quick way to pool what a team knows about a user: says, thinks, does, feels. *In a prompt:* "Build an empathy map for this user from the interview notes."
- **[Personas Make Users Memorable](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/persona/)** (NN/g; article; free). What a persona is for, what belongs in one, and how to stop it becoming fiction. *In a prompt:* "Write a persona grounded only in what the research actually showed."
- **[User Need Statements](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-need-statements/)** (NN/g; article; free). The formula for a problem statement: a user needs a way to do something, so that they get a benefit. *In a prompt:* "Write three user need statements in the form: user, need, goal."
- **[Task Analysis](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/task-analysis/)** (NN/g; article; free). How to break a goal into the steps a person actually takes, before designing a flow for it. *In a prompt:* "Break this goal into tasks and subtasks, as the user sees them."
- **[Tree Testing](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tree-testing/)** (NN/g; article; free). How to test whether people can find things in your menu structure, without any visual design. *In a prompt:* "Write eight tree-test tasks for this navigation structure."
- **[Wireflows](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wireflows/)** (NN/g; article; free). A deliverable that combines wireframes with flow arrows. Often the best format for app screens. *In a prompt:* "Present this as a wireflow: small wireframes joined by arrows labelled with the trigger."
- **[UX Prototypes: Low Fidelity vs. High Fidelity](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-prototype-hi-lo-fidelity/)** (NN/g; article; free). What fidelity means across interactivity, visuals and content, and how to choose the right level. *In a prompt:* "What is the lowest fidelity that would answer the question we are testing?"
- **[Design Critiques](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/design-critiques/)** (NN/g; article; free). How to give and receive critique against goals, not taste, with roles and rules. *In a prompt:* "Critique this against its stated goals. No opinions without a reason."
- **[How we do design critiques at Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/design-critiques-at-figma/)** (Noah Levin · Figma; article; free). Several critique formats used by a leading design team, and when each one fits. *In a prompt:* "Run this as a silent critique: list written observations first, then discuss."
- **[The New User Story Backlog is a Map](https://jpattonassociates.com/the-new-backlog/)** (Jeff Patton; article; free). The origin of user story mapping: arrange stories along the user's journey to see what a first release must include. *In a prompt:* "Lay out a story map: activities across the top, and the thinnest usable release beneath."
- **[Continuous Discovery Habits](https://www.producttalk.org/continuous-discovery-habits/)** (Teresa Torres; book; paid). The standard book on weekly customer contact and deciding what to build from it. *In a prompt:* "Set up a weekly discovery habit for a team of one."
- **[Lean UX and other books](https://jeffgothelf.com/books/)** (Jeff Gothelf & Josh Seiden; book; paid). Lean UX treats every design as a hypothesis to test. The source of 'outcomes over outputs'. *In a prompt:* "State this feature as a hypothesis: we believe, for whom, and how we will know."
- **[Interviewing Users](https://portigal.com/books/)** (Steve Portigal; book; paid). The most complete book on the craft of the research interview. *In a prompt:* "What follow-up probes would a skilled interviewer use here?"
- **[Rocket Surgery Made Easy](https://sensible.com/rocket-surgery-made-easy/)** (Steve Krug; book; paid). A do-it-yourself guide to running usability tests once a month with three people. *In a prompt:* "Plan a morning of do-it-yourself testing with three participants."
- **[SVPG books](https://www.svpg.com/books/)** (Marty Cagan · Silicon Valley Product Group; book; paid). Inspired and its successors: how strong product teams discover and deliver. *In a prompt:* "Describe how an empowered product team would approach this problem."
- **[UX Basics: Study Guide](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-basics-study-guide/)** (NN/g; reference; free). A structured reading list for newcomers, linking NN/g's introductory articles in order. *In a prompt:* "Give me a reading order for learning the basics of this topic."
- **[Psychology for UX: Study Guide](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/psychology-study-guide/)** (NN/g; reference; free). An index of articles on attention, memory, perception and decision-making as they apply to design. *In a prompt:* "Which psychological principle explains why users miss this element?"
