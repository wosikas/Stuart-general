# Design resources - Designing AI features

Part of [[11 Resource library]] in the [[Product Design Prompt Kit]].

Guidance for features where the product itself uses AI.

12 links. A star marks an essential. Last link check: 18 November 2026.

- **[People + AI Guidebook](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook/)** ★ (Google PAIR; guideline; free). The most complete guide to designing AI features: mental models, explainability, feedback, and failing gracefully. *In a prompt:* "Design this AI feature so users can see why it suggested something and correct it."
- **[Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/)** ★ (Microsoft HAX Toolkit; guideline; free). Eighteen guidelines grouped by moment: initially, during interaction, when wrong, over time. *In a prompt:* "Check this AI feature against the 18 Human-AI Interaction guidelines."
- **[The Shape of AI](https://www.shapeof.ai/)** (Emily Campbell; reference; free). A pattern library for AI interfaces: suggestions, citations, regeneration, controls and trust markers. *In a prompt:* "Which Shape of AI patterns fit a 'suggest, don't decide' assistant?"
- **[Apple HIG: Generative AI](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)** (Apple; guideline; free). Apple's guidance on designing responsible generative features: transparency, control and handling errors. *In a prompt:* "Keep people in control: show AI output as a suggestion they accept or dismiss."
- **[Apple HIG: Machine learning](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)** (Apple; guideline; free). How to design around predictions: confidence, corrections, implicit and explicit feedback. *In a prompt:* "Design for wrong predictions: easy correction and no penalty."
- **[Carbon for AI](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/carbon-for-ai)** (IBM; guideline; free). How a design system marks AI-generated content and exposes explanations consistently. *In a prompt:* "Mark AI-generated content with a consistent label and an explanation on demand."
- **[NN/g: Artificial Intelligence](https://www.nngroup.com/topic/ai/)** (NN/g; reference; free). Research on how people actually use AI products, including prompt behaviours and trust. *In a prompt:* "What does usability research say about how people really use AI assistants?"
- **[AI for UX: Getting Started](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-ux-getting-started/)** (NN/g; article; free). A practical introduction to using AI tools in design work, and where they help or mislead. *In a prompt:* "Where in this project is AI output trustworthy, and where must I check it?"
- **[Generative UI and Outcome-Oriented Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/generative-ui/)** (NN/g; article; free). What happens to design when interfaces are generated for each user in the moment. *In a prompt:* "Define the outcome and constraints, and let the layout vary within them."
- **[Design for the AI age](https://linear.app/now/design-for-the-ai-age)** (Linear; article; free). A design-led company's view of how interfaces change when software can act on its own. *In a prompt:* "Where should the AI act alone, and where should it only propose?"
- **[Language Model Sketchbook, or Why I Hate Chatbots](https://maggieappleton.com/lm-sketchbook)** (Maggie Appleton; article; free). Illustrated ideas for AI features that are not chat boxes, such as critics and assistants in the margins of your writing. *In a prompt:* "Propose three AI interfaces for this feature that are not a chat box."
- **[Squish Meets Structure](https://maggieappleton.com/squish-structure)** (Maggie Appleton; article; free). A talk on designing with language models: where flexible, fuzzy AI meets rigid software structure. *In a prompt:* "Which parts of this should be fixed structure, and which should be flexible AI?"
