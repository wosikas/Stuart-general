# Resource library

Part of [[Product Design Prompt Kit]].

428 hand-picked links: the guidelines, landmark articles, top websites, books, galleries, tools and people that working product designers rely on. Each entry ends with a line you can paste into a prompt. A star marks the 47 essentials.

Last link check: 18 November 2026. On that day 418 of the 428 links were confirmed live. The other ten sites block automated checks.

## Start here, in this order

1. **See the problems.** Learn the ten rules every design critique is scored against. [10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)
2. **Learn the names.** Pick up the psychology principles designers cite by name. [Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/)
3. **Judge the visuals.** Two free reads that turn "it looks off" into specific, fixable rules. [7 Rules for Creating Gorgeous UI](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html) and [Visual design rules you can safely follow every time](https://anthonyhobday.com/sideprojects/saferules/)
4. **Know the platform rules.** Read the comparison once, then use the guidelines as lookups. [iOS vs. Android App UI Design: The Complete Guide](https://www.learnui.design/blog/ios-vs-android-app-ui-design-complete-guide.html), [Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/) and [Material Design 3](https://m3.material.io/)
5. **Collect references.** Find how real products solved your problem, and learn what each component is called. [Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/) and [The Component Gallery](https://component.gallery/)
6. **Direct the AI.** Read the official prompting guide, then practise with [[05 Prompt templates]] and judge each result with [[09 Review checklist]]. [Prompt engineering overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/overview)

## The essentials

### Foundations

- **[Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/)** ★ (Jon Yablonski; reference; free). The psychology principles behind good interfaces, one clear page each. The quickest way to learn the names designers cite. *In a prompt:* "Which Laws of UX does this screen break? Name each one and fix it."
- **[10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)** ★ (Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). The ten rules every heuristic review is scored against, with examples. Read it before you ask an AI to critique anything. *In a prompt:* "Review this flow against Nielsen's 10 heuristics, with severity from 0 to 4."
- **[Don't Make Me Think](https://sensible.com/dont-make-me-think/)** ★ (Steve Krug; book; paid). Usability basics in a weekend read. It trains you to notice friction, which is what makes your feedback prompts sharp. *In a prompt:* "Apply Krug's first law: what on this screen makes a new user stop and think?"
- **[Refactoring UI](https://www.refactoringui.com/)** ★ (Adam Wathan & Steve Schoger; book; paid). Visual design tactics for people who aren't designers: hierarchy, spacing, colour, depth. The fastest route to judging UI quality. *In a prompt:* "De-emphasise the secondary content instead of emphasising the primary action further."
- **[7 Rules for Creating Gorgeous UI](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html)** ★ (Erik Kennedy; article; free). A free two-part crash course in visual design, written by an engineer who taught himself. Covers light, contrast, whitespace and text over images. *In a prompt:* "Light comes from the sky: check that every shadow and highlight is consistent."
- **[Growth.design case studies](https://growth.design/case-studies)** ★ (Dan Benoni & Louis-Xavier Lavallée; case studies; free). Comic-style teardowns of real product flows, each tied to named psychology principles. Enjoyable and dense with vocabulary. *In a prompt:* "Tear down our onboarding in the style of a Growth.design case study."

### Landmark articles

- **[Dieter Rams: ten principles for good design](https://www.vitsoe.com/gb/about/good-design)** ★ (Dieter Rams · Vitsœ; article; free). The ten principles behind Braun's products, which later shaped Apple's. The last one, 'as little design as possible', is the one designers quote most. *In a prompt:* "Apply 'good design is as little design as possible': what can be removed from this screen?"
- **[The $300 Million Button](https://articles.centercentre.com/three_hund_million_button/)** ★ (Jared Spool; article; free). How removing a forced registration step from a checkout added $300 million a year. The classic proof that one small flow decision can outweigh a redesign. *In a prompt:* "Where does this flow force a decision before the user has what they came for?"
- **[Never Use a Warning When you Mean Undo](https://alistapart.com/article/neveruseawarning/)** ★ (Aza Raskin · A List Apart; article; free). Why people click through warnings without reading them, and why undo is the respectful alternative. The source of 'undo over confirm' in this kit. *In a prompt:* "Replace every confirmation dialog on a reversible action with undo."
- **[You Are Not the User: The False-Consensus Effect](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/false-consensus/)** ★ (NN/g; article; free). Why your own preferences are poor evidence of what users want. The most important idea for anyone designing a product they also use. *In a prompt:* "List the assumptions here that rest only on my own habits, and how to test each."
- **[AI: First New UI Paradigm in 60 Years](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-paradigm/)** ★ (Jakob Nielsen · NN/g; article; free). Argues that telling a computer what outcome you want, rather than what steps to take, is a new kind of interface. It explains why prompting is a design skill. *In a prompt:* "Describe the outcome I want and let me check it, instead of asking me for each step."
- **[The Articulation Barrier](https://www.uxtigers.com/post/ai-articulation-barrier)** ★ (Jakob Nielsen · UX Tigers; article; free). Why describing what you want in prose is hard for most people, and how that limits AI tools. It names the problem this whole kit exists to solve. *In a prompt:* "Offer me choices to pick from wherever I would otherwise have to describe something in words."
- **[Designing accessible color systems](https://stripe.com/blog/accessible-color-systems)** ★ (Daryl Koopersmith & Wilson Miner · Stripe; article; free). How Stripe built a palette where every colour at the same step has the same contrast. The model for any modern colour system. *In a prompt:* "Build the palette so every hue at the same step has the same contrast against white."

### Top design websites

- **[A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/)** ★ (A List Apart; website; free). The magazine where responsive design, mobile-first forms and much of modern web practice were first argued in print. Deep, edited, lasting articles. *In a prompt:* "What has A List Apart published on this topic, and what is the core argument?"
- **[Google Design](https://design.google/)** ★ (Google; website; free). Case studies, interviews and resources from Google's design teams, including the thinking behind Material. *In a prompt:* "How did Google's team approach a problem like this one?"

### Platform guidelines

- **[Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/)** ★ (Apple; guideline; free). The rulebook for iOS, iPadOS and macOS. Read Foundations first, then the component page for whatever you are designing. *In a prompt:* "Follow Apple HIG for sheets: detents, grabber, and dismissal behaviour."
- **[Material Design 3](https://m3.material.io/)** ★ (Google; guideline; free). The rulebook for Android and a strong general reference. Each component page has anatomy, specs and do/don't examples. *In a prompt:* "Use Material 3 components and tokens; cite the component spec you followed."
- **[iOS vs. Android App UI Design: The Complete Guide](https://www.learnui.design/blog/ios-vs-android-app-ui-design-complete-guide.html)** ★ (Erik Kennedy; article; free). A side-by-side comparison of navigation, controls, typography and conventions on the two platforms. Expands the table in this kit. *In a prompt:* "List every place this iOS design must change to feel native on Android."

### Component and pattern guides

- **[Website Forms Usability: Top 10 Recommendations](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-form-design/)** ★ (NN/g; article; free). Ten rules that cover most form mistakes: keep it short, one column, labels above, group related fields, mark optional fields. *In a prompt:* "Apply NN/g's ten form recommendations and tell me which ones this form breaks."
- **[Design better data tables](https://mattstromawn.com/writing/tables/)** ★ (Matt Ström-Awn; article; free). The best short guide to tables: align numbers right, text left, use tabular figures, and remove most of the lines. *In a prompt:* "Right-align numbers, left-align text, use tabular figures, and remove vertical rules."
- **[Smart Interface Design Patterns](https://smart-interface-design-patterns.com/)** ★ (Vitaly Friedman; course; free tier). Evidence-based guidelines and checklists for dozens of specific patterns, from mega-menus to date pickers. The articles are free; the video course is paid. *In a prompt:* "Give me a design checklist for this pattern, with the edge cases people forget."

### Design systems

- **[GOV.UK Design System](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/)** ★ (UK Government Digital Service; guideline; free). Every component and pattern comes with the research behind it. The best example of plain, accessible, tested design. *In a prompt:* "Use the GOV.UK 'one thing per page' pattern for this multi-step form."
- **[shadcn/ui](https://ui.shadcn.com/)** ★ (shadcn; kit; free). The component set most AI builders reach for by default. Knowing its component names lets you ask for them directly. *In a prompt:* "Build with shadcn/ui: Sheet, Command, Toast and Skeleton. No custom components."
- **[The Component Gallery](https://component.gallery/)** ★ (Iain Bean; reference; free). Every common component, what it is called in each major design system, and links to all their versions. Invaluable for naming things. *In a prompt:* "What do the major design systems call this pattern, and how do they handle it?"

### Pattern and flow galleries

- **[Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/)** ★ (Mobbin; gallery; free tier). The largest searchable library of real app screens and full flows, for iOS, Android and web. The best source of references to name in prompts. *In a prompt:* "Model the capture flow on how Things 3 and Bear handle quick entry."

### Research and strategy

- **[The Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/)** ★ (Rob Fitzpatrick; book; paid). How to ask customers questions they can't answer politely and falsely. The best short book on interviewing. *In a prompt:* "Rewrite these interview questions so they pass the Mom Test."
- **[UX Research Cheat Sheet](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-research-cheat-sheet/)** ★ (NN/g; article; free). Which research method to use at each stage of a project, on one page. *In a prompt:* "Which research method fits where we are, and what would it cost us to skip it?"
- **[When to Use Which User-Experience Research Methods](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/which-ux-research-methods/)** ★ (Christian Rohrer · NN/g; article; free). The standard map of twenty research methods, placed on three axes. Tells you which method answers which kind of question. *In a prompt:* "Which research method answers this question: what people do, or what they say?"
- **[Articulating Design Decisions](https://tomgreever.com/articulating-design-decisions-book/)** ★ (Tom Greever; book; paid). How to explain and defend design choices to people who are not designers. Directly useful for asking an AI to justify its choices, and for judging the answer. *In a prompt:* "For each decision, say what problem it solves, how it affects the user, and why it beats the alternative."

### Visual craft

- **[Visual design rules you can safely follow every time](https://anthonyhobday.com/sideprojects/saferules/)** ★ (Anthony Hobday; article; free). About thirty concrete rules, each with a picture: near-black not black, spacing in multiples, nested corner radii. Perfect for turning into prompt constraints. *In a prompt:* "Follow Anthony Hobday's safe rules; list any you chose to break."
- **[Practical Typography](https://practicaltypography.com/)** ★ (Matthew Butterick; book; free). Read 'Typography in ten minutes' and 'Summary of key rules'. They cover line length, line spacing and font choice, which is most of what matters. *In a prompt:* "Keep body text to 45 to 90 characters per line with line spacing of 120 to 145%."
- **[Building Your Color Palette](https://www.refactoringui.com/previews/building-your-color-palette)** ★ (Adam Wathan & Steve Schoger; article; free). A free chapter of Refactoring UI. Explains why you need eight to ten shades of grey, a primary colour and several accents, each in nine steps. *In a prompt:* "Define greys, one primary and three accent colours, each as a nine-step scale from 100 to 900."

### Interaction and motion

- **[Designing Fluid Interfaces](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)** ★ (Apple · WWDC 2018; video; free). The talk that explains why iOS gestures feel right: interruptible, direct and responsive from the first frame. *In a prompt:* "Make the sheet interruptible and redirectable mid-gesture."

### Content and UX writing

- **[Mailchimp Content Style Guide](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/)** ★ (Mailchimp; guideline; free). The model voice-and-tone guide. Shows how to stay one personality while adapting tone to the reader's mood. *In a prompt:* "Write in a voice that is plain and warm, never cute. Adapt tone to error versus success."
- **[Error-Message Guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/)** ★ (NN/g; article; free). How to write errors that are visible, precise and constructive, with a scoring rubric. *In a prompt:* "Each error says what happened and how to fix it, next to the field, with no blame."

### Accessibility

- **[WCAG 2.2 Quick Reference](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/)** ★ (W3C; guideline; free). Every success criterion, filterable by level, with techniques for meeting each. The source you cite in audits. *In a prompt:* "Audit against WCAG 2.2 AA and cite the success criterion number for each issue."
- **[Designing for Web Accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tips/designing/)** ★ (W3C WAI; guideline; free). Ten illustrated tips that cover most accessibility issues a designer controls. *In a prompt:* "Check this design against the W3C's ten tips for designing for accessibility."

### Designing AI features

- **[People + AI Guidebook](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook/)** ★ (Google PAIR; guideline; free). The most complete guide to designing AI features: mental models, explainability, feedback, and failing gracefully. *In a prompt:* "Design this AI feature so users can see why it suggested something and correct it."
- **[Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/)** ★ (Microsoft HAX Toolkit; guideline; free). Eighteen guidelines grouped by moment: initially, during interaction, when wrong, over time. *In a prompt:* "Check this AI feature against the 18 Human-AI Interaction guidelines."

### AI tools and prompting

- **[Prompt engineering overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/overview)** ★ (Anthropic; guideline; free). The official guide to prompting Claude: be clear and direct, give examples, assign a role, let it think. *In a prompt:* "Before answering, think through the user's goal and the constraints step by step."
- **[Mermaid Live Editor](https://mermaid.live/)** ★ (Mermaid; tool; free). Paste the Mermaid flowchart that the user flow template produces and see it drawn instantly. *In a prompt:* "Output the flow as a Mermaid flowchart I can paste into mermaid.live."
- **[CARE: Structure for Crafting AI Prompts](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/careful-prompts/)** ★ (NN/g; article; free). A four-part prompt structure written for UX work: context, ask, rules, examples. A simpler cousin of the anatomy in this kit. *In a prompt:* "Structure the prompt as context, ask, rules and examples."

### Courses and practice

- **[Practical UI](https://www.practical-ui.com/)** ★ (Adham Dannaway; book; paid). A logic-driven visual design handbook with clear rules and before-and-after examples. A strong companion to Refactoring UI. *In a prompt:* "Give a logical reason for every visual decision, not a taste judgement."

### Voices to follow

- **[LukeW](https://www.lukew.com/ff/)** ★ (Luke Wroblewski; blog; free). Two decades of short, evidence-led notes on forms, mobile and now AI interfaces. *In a prompt:* "Apply mobile-first thinking: what stays when the screen is small?"
- **[Dive Club](https://www.dive.club/)** ★ (Ridd; podcast; free). Interviews with leading product designers about how they actually work, including how they use AI tools. *In a prompt:* "How are top product designers fitting AI tools into their process?"

### Notes and knowledge tools

- **[Local-first software](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/local-first/)** ★ (Ink & Switch; article; free). The essay that set out seven ideals for software where you own your data and it works offline. Directly relevant to sync and export. *In a prompt:* "Assess this design against the seven local-first ideals."
- **[Maggie Appleton](https://maggieappleton.com/)** ★ (Maggie Appleton; blog; free). Illustrated essays on digital gardens, tools for thought, and interfaces for language models. *In a prompt:* "Design the AI as a collaborator in the margins, not an author."
