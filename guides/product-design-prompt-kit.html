Product Design Prompt Kit

Experienced product designers don't ask for "a nice screen". They name the user, the job, the platform, the states and the stage of work, then judge the result against known principles. This kit gives you that vocabulary, the order to work in, and prompts you can copy.

The running example is a quick-capture sheet for Loom: jot a note in seconds, with no filing decision. Swap in your own feature wherever you see brackets.

01 · Mental model

Work from the bottom plane up

Jesse James Garrett's five planes are how designers sequence decisions. Each plane constrains the one above it. Most weak AI output comes from asking for the top plane while the lower ones are still blank.

Concrete · what people seeUI
Abstract · why it existsUX

The rule: write one prompt per plane and feed each answer into the next. The strategy answer becomes context for the flow prompt. The flow becomes input for the wireframe prompt. This habit, more than any vocabulary, is what makes prompting sound like a designer.

02 · Artefacts

What designers create, stage by stage

Designers follow the Double Diamond: explore the problem widely, converge on the real one, explore solutions widely, converge on one. Each stage produces named artefacts. Asking for an artefact by name is the quickest way to sound like a designer.

03 · Structure

Anatomy of a designer's prompt

The difference isn't fancy words. Every decision the AI would otherwise guess has already been made.

How most people ask
Design a quick capture screen for my notes app. Make it modern and clean.

The AI must guess the user, the platform, the entry point, what "done" means, which states matter and what to hand back. It guesses the average, which is why the result looks generic.
