GGlossary: the terms in this guide

You only need a term if it helps you explain an effect. Examiners reward the effect, not the label.
TermWhat it meansExample from the poems
AlliterationWords close together starting with the same sound“sneer of cold command” (Ozymandias)
SibilanceRepeated “s” sounds, often hissing or soft“Sudden successive flights of bullets streak the silence” (Exposure)
MetaphorDescribing one thing as if it were another“Space is a salvo,” (Storm on the Island)
SimileA comparison using “like” or “as”“spits like a tame cat” (Storm on the Island)
PersonificationGiving something non-human human actions or feelingsthe mountain “Strode after me.” (The Prelude)
ImperativeA command“Look on my works, ye Mighty, and despair!” (Ozymandias)
Oxymoron / paradoxTwo opposite ideas put together“marriage hearse” (London); “a huge nothing” (Storm on the Island)
IronyWhen the result is the opposite of what someone meant or expectedthe boast followed by “Nothing beside remains.” (Ozymandias)
EnjambmentA sentence running on over a line break“a one-way / journey into history” (Kamikaze)
CaesuraA pause or full stop in the middle of a line“Belfast. Beirut. Phnom Penh.” (War Photographer)
RefrainA line repeated through a poem“But nothing happens.” (Exposure)
VoltaThe “turn” in a poem, where the thought changes direction“End of story, except not really.” (Remains)
SonnetA 14-line poem, usually in iambic pentameter with a set rhyme patternOzymandias
Blank verseUnrhymed lines of about ten syllables with a regular beatExtract from The Prelude
Dramatic monologueA poem spoken by one character who is not the poet, often to a silent listenerMy Last Duchess
Free versePoetry with no regular rhyme or rhythmPoppies
DialectThe way a language is spoken in a particular place or community“Dem tell me” (Checking Out Me History)
Romantic poetsA group of poets around 1800, including Wordsworth and Shelley. Britannica says Lyrical Ballads (1798) “helped launch the English Romantic movement”.Ozymandias, The Prelude
