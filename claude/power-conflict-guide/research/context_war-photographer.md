## 5. Form (counted from anth.txt + one source)

- 24 lines, 4 stanzas × 6 lines (counted). Third person ("he") throughout (text). WJEC-WP: "The poem is written in third person to reflect the detachment a war photographer must feel when doing the job." and "There are four stanzas with six lines each, suggesting regularity and the routine nature of his job. Each stanza has a similar and consistent rhyme scheme (ABBCDD)" and "The strict, controlled structure contrasts the chaos, confusion and lack of structure in war."
- Our count of line-end words in anth.txt: st.1 alone / rows / glows / he / Mass / grass; st.2 trays / then / again / dispel / feet / heat; st.3 features / eyes / cries / approval / must / dust; st.4 black-and-white / six / prick / beers / where / care. So: lines 2–3 rhyme or half-rhyme in every stanza (rows/glows, then/again, eyes/cries, six/prick); every stanza ends with a rhyming couplet (Mass/grass, feet/heat, must/dust, where/care). Note: anth.txt breaks line 9 after "Home again"; AQA-J19-QP (per verify3) ended the line differently, so follow anth.txt.
- Inside the lines: also internal rhyme "tears … beers" (l.22) (text).

## 6. AQA's own angle (from exam_materials.md, verbatim)

- June 2019 Q26: "Compare how poets present the ways that people are affected by war in ‘War Photographer’ and in one other poem from ‘Power and conflict’."
- MS 2019 AO3: "any ideas about the impact/influence of experiences on the individual/collective conscience"; "any valid exploration of ideas about bearing witness"; "ideas about the individual and the state, possibly linked to morality/duty".
- MS 2019 AO1: "people indirectly affected by war, such as in ‘Poppies’ or ‘The Emigrée’"; "those with a different perspective, such as ‘Bayonet Charge’ or ‘Exposure’ or ‘Remains’".
- MS 2019 AO2: "presentation of perspective, such as contrast between ‘hands’, ‘eyes’, ‘aeroplane’".
- MS-J22 AO2: "more reflective stance of ‘War Photographer’ or ‘Poppies’". ER-N20: "‘War Photographer’ was used by many students".

## Gaps and conflicts

1. Philip Jones Griffiths as an inspiration: WJEC only. McCullin: WJEC + Oak ("may be"). No Duffy interview opened; the "dilemma of someone who has that as a job" quotation is untraced (GAP, not used).
2. That the "running children in a nightmare heat" recalls Nick Ut's 1972 napalm photograph: PMT only, plus WJEC's "Interview with Nick Ut" further-reading link. Pulitzer (403) and World Press Photo (no caption) could not confirm. Not used as fact.
3. Cambodia death toll: Britannica 1.5–3 million vs WJEC "almost 3 million".
4. Tate Liverpool quotes came through the fetch tool and may be section texts by Tate, not McCullin's own words, except where quotation marks show speech; treat as Tate.
5. Text variants (from verify3): AQA "darkroom", "black-and-white", "pick out"; SPL "dark room", "black and white"; WJEC "dark room", "pick five or six". Follow anth.txt.
6. No source found on "Sunday supplements" as a 1980s media phenomenon beyond Tate/DWB's references to the *Sunday Times Magazine*.
