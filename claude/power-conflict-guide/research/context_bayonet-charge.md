## 5. AQA on this poem (from pc/exam_materials.md, verbatim)
- June 2017 Q: "Compare how poets present the effects of war in ‘Bayonet Charge’ and in one other poem". MS17 AO3: "Ideas about the realisation of the horror / reality of war"; "Any treatment of the relationship between the individual soldiers and those for whom they fought, e.g. comparison with ‘The Charge of the Light Brigade’ or ‘Exposure’". AO1: "Any valid comparison of the effects of war on the individual, such as ‘Remains’ or ‘Exposure’".
- June 2022 Q: "ideas about conflict". ER-J22: in media res, "suddenly", "raw", "sweating", "centre of his chest", "patriotic tear", "king, honour…", "cold clockwork", "numb as a smashed arm", "yellow hare", "shot-slashed furrows"; nature link with Exposure "less confident"; Tennyson "six hundred" vs anonymous soldier; warnings on enjambment/caesura lists and ABAB rhyme.
- Nov 2020 MS AO2 says "first person in ‘Bayonet Charge’": **an AQA slip**. The poem is third person ("he").

## Form (our count from anth.txt)
23 lines, three stanzas of 8 / 7 / 8. No regular rhyme or metre; lines of uneven length. Third person, past tense, one unnamed soldier. No allowed source opened names the form ("free verse" is PMT's wording only), so describe it rather than label it.
