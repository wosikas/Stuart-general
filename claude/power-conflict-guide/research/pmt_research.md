## 2. How each PMT per-poem PDF is structured [PMT]

The template is the same in all 15 PDFs:

1. **Cover page:** "AQA English GCSE / Poetry: Power and Conflict / <Poem> - <Poet>", plus the CC licence.
2. **Title banner** (poem title in capitals and the poet).
3. **Brief Summary:** 2–3 sentences on what the poem is about and its central idea.
4. **Synopsis:** about 5–9 bullet points that retell the poem in order.
5. **Context:** a poet bio subheading with dates (e.g. "Seamus Heaney (1939-2013)"), then 1–3 named context subheadings (e.g. "Romantic Era", "Historical influences in the poem"; "Written for 'The Not Dead' (2007)"; "From 'Death of a Naturalist' (1966)"; "The Crimean War"; "French Revolution / Industrial Revolution"; "Kamikaze Pilots"). Some include a boxed "<Poem>" overview paragraph that links context to the power or conflict theme.
6. **Annotated poem:** the full poem text in the centre column, with key words in bold and margin call-outs on both sides. **PMT reprints the full text of in-copyright poems** (e.g. Heaney, Armitage). We must not do this.
7. **The title "<Poem>":** what the title means.
8. **Perspective:** voice and speaker, often subheaded "Dramatic Monologue" (Remains, Storm, London, MLD), "Shifts in narrative perspective" (Kamikaze).
9. **The opening / The Opening** and sometimes **The ending / The last stanza / Last word**, each with a short quotation and its analysis.
10. **Structure** (sometimes "Form" first, or "Structure and form"). Subheadings name the device, e.g. Rhyme Scheme, Sonnet, Iambic Pentameter, Cyclical Structure, Volta, Enjambment, Caesura, Pararhymes, Anaphora, Ellipses, Ballad form, Dactylic dimeter, Free verse, Dual structure, Stanza lengths, "Shifting blame", "Rigid form". Boxed definitions are styled like "IAMBIC PENTAMETER | ..." and "MOTIF | A recurring theme in a text."
11. **Language:** subheadings by device or idea, e.g. Alliteration, Consonance, Metaphors, Symbolism, Similes, Personification, Colloquialisms, Sibilance, Semantic Fields, Juxtaposition, Euphemisms, Epistrophe, Subjunctive case, Extended metaphor, Possessive pronouns, "Tools of War".
12. **A thematic "Focus on..." block,** with a different name in each PDF:
    - Ozymandias: "Focus on Power and Pride" and "Shelley's Message"
    - Remains: "Memory / Psychological Impact", "Guilt"
    - Storm on the Island: "Focus on natural language", "Isolation vs Community"
    - Exposure: "Religious references", "Personification of nature", "Reality of war", "Boredom", "Soldiers are forgotten", "Suffering", "Fear"
    - War Photographer: "Themes": "Response to conflict", "Suffering", "Impact on civilians", "The extensive scale of conflict", "Futility"
    - Kamikaze: "Language and Themes": "Conflict vs Identity", "Roles of Soldier vs Father", "Theme of 'Those Left Behind'"
    - London: "Suffering", "Symbolism"
    - Tissue: "Religion", "Society", "Human power"
    - Poppies: "Blending domestic with conflict", "Unconventional bravery", "Suffering"
    - My Last Duchess: "Duchess as a painting", "Statue of Neptune", "Power"
    - Bayonet Charge: "Reality of war", "Nature", "The hare", "The surroundings", "Allusion to Owen", "Style", "Can't describe war himself"
    - The Prelude: "Loss of Eloquence", "Personification of Nature", "Semantic Fields", "Mythology"
    - CMH: "Metaphors", "Tone"
13. **Comparisons:** usually a two-column table ("<Poem> | <Other poem>") with a one-line "Both..." summary under it, then 1–3 headed pairings ("<Poem> & <Other>") laid out as **Similarities** and **Differences** bullet blocks. Each bullet makes a point and gives a short quote from each poem.

There is **no separate "key quotes" list.** Quotes appear only in the margin annotations and in the analysis.

Of the sections in our planned layout, PMT has: summary (Brief Summary and Synopsis), context, perspective, form/structure, language, themes (partly, as "Focus on" blocks), and comparisons. PMT does **not** have: key-quotes tables, exam-question practice, AO mapping, or grade-boundary or model-paragraph guidance.

## 4. Exam technique [PMT] (verbatim)

### Q26 (anthology comparison)
**PMT gives no Q26 exam-technique advice on the Power and Conflict page or in any of its PDFs.** The PDFs offer only content and comparison points. Its blog link covers unseen poetry only.

### Unseen poetry: PMT Unseen Poetry Guide PDF
URL: https://pmt.physicsandmathstutor.com/download/English-Literature/GCSE/Notes/AQA/Poetry/Unseen-Poetry/Unseen%20Poetry%20Guide%20-%20Section%20C%20-%20AQA%20English%20Literature%20GCSE.pdf
PMT never uses question numbers (27.1/27.2). It calls them "the 24 mark question" / "Question 1" and "the 8 marker".

Overview page:
> "24 marks for the first question focusing exclusively on the initial unseen poem."
> "8 marks for the second question, which focuses on comparing it to another unseen poem."
> "The difference between unseen poetry and the anthology section on the paper is that there are no marks awarded for both context and understanding the tone of the poems presented to you."
> "However, those marking your paper are checking to see if you've examined the meaning behind the text, and then how the author has used structure, form, imagery and language to create this meaning in their poem."
> "You are allocated around 45 minutes for Paper 2 Section C. It's probably a good idea to spend 30-35 minutes on the 24 mark question, leaving your remaining time for the shorter 8 marker."

"[24] How to Tackle Question 1 [24]":
> "When faced with the poem, stay calm and initially read it over once. There's no need to start annotating until you have got the basic gist of the poem."
> "If you don't understand it the first time you read it keep the question in mind and start thinking about the connotations of the title and spotting poetic devices (such as metaphors, similes, sibilance, etc)."
> "When annotating make sure that you are only labelling devices that you have something to write about, if you annotate every single thing you find then you'll run out of time to write your essay."
> "Remember to always keep thinking about the question, and the theme or perspective it's asking you to explore, in relation to the poem."
> "Once you have come up with 2 - 4 points about the poem (though three solid points tend to work effectively for most people), you can analyse the structure. You should note things like the rhyme scheme used and syllable line length, and you can then link these to your points about the theme / perspectives presented in the poem."
> "Also, gather key quotes which link to each of your points before you begin writing your essay. These can become paragraphs and mean you will have a clear structure in front of you to work from."
> "For each of your paragraphs in your essay, try and include:" "1-3 quotes which support your point from the poem." / "Detailed analysis of at least one of your quotes, with poetic devices and the meaning implied from it included." / "The effect on a potential reader and the reason the author may have used the language." / "A comment on the structure of the poem which supports your point and links back to the question." / "Different interpretations of both the quote and structural point, which also link back to the overarching point you're making in the paragraph and the question."

"Tackling an Exam Question": a worked example using the public-domain poem "A Dream Within A Dream" (Q: "How does Allen Poe present time in 'A Dream within a Dream'?"). It uses three analytical categories: "Content and Ideas", "Structure and Form", "Language".

8-mark comparison:
> "As you have such a little amount of allocated time for this short question, a brief plan (if any) will suffice, but start writing as soon as possible."
> "Focus on always comparing similarities or differences between the two given poems rather going into any solidly detailed analysis of just one or the other - remember, you have very limited time."
> "Try and write three short, concise paragraphs - or two longer ones if you're going to go into more specific detail - on two comparative points."
> "A good guideline to start planning this sort of question would be to plan two paragraphs of similarities and one paragraph of difference between the two poems."
> "Try to include a reference to structure in one of the paragraphs. However, the key is to include a quote for each comparative point for each of the poem, along with a specific poetic technique the writer is using."
> "Also remember to include how the poetic techniques are used and how the message the writer is trying to convey affects the reader."

The last page, "What could you write about for structure and form and language?", is a device glossary. Structure devices: Extended Metaphors, Enjambment, Semantic Fields, Symbols, Extended Allegory, Line Breaks, Line/Stanza Length, Caesura, Speaker, Narrative Style, Narrative Tone, Poetic Form, Meter, Rhyming Scheme, Flashbacks, Chronological order, Epigraph, In medias res, Syntactic sentence structures. Language devices: about 37 terms from Anaphora to Zoomorphism, each with a one-line example.

### Unseen poetry: PMT blog
URL: https://www.pmt.education/blog/students/aqa-gcse-unseen-poetry-guide/
Headings: What is unseen poetry? / How to unpick an unseen poem / How to respond to the unseen poetry single poem analysis / How to respond to the unseen poetry comparison question / FAQs.
> "The unseen poetry section is on Paper 2, Section C of the AQA GCSE English Literature exam."
> "It consists of two questions; the first (worth 24 marks) asks you to analyse how a given theme or concept is presented in just one poem, and the second (worth 8 marks) asks you to compare two unseen poems."

## 5. Possible conflicts with AQA materials or errors (noted, not resolved) [PMT]

- **Tone and "no marks":** the PMT guide says there are "no marks awarded for both context and understanding the tone". AQA's unseen questions assess AO1 and AO2 only (no AO3/context), but AQA does not exclude tone, which can count as a writer's method or effect under AO2. Check against the AQA mark scheme.
- **8-mark paragraph count:** the PMT guide says "three short, concise paragraphs - or two longer ones" (2 similarities + 1 difference). The PMT blog says "2 clear comparison paragraphs". PMT contradicts itself. AQA prescribes no number of paragraphs.
- **"Allocated around 45 minutes"** is presented as an allocation. AQA gives no per-section timing on the paper. This is a recommendation, not a rule.
- **Question numbering:** PMT calls them "Question 1/2". AQA papers number them 27.1 and 27.2, and Q26 is the anthology question.
- **Ozymandias PDF:** gives Shelley's dates as "(1772-1822)". He was born in 1792. Ramesses II's dates are given as "1279-1239 BC" (the usual dates are c.1279–1213 BC). It also says he was "defeated by the 12 tribes of Israel", which is doubtful. It calls the speaker both first-person and "omniscient".
- **Remains PDF:** the title page banner reads "MOTHER ANY DISTANCE" (a different Armitage poem), which looks like a template error. It describes "legs it", "tosses" and "are" as "present participles".
- **Remains video link** on the P&C page goes to a BBC clip whose URL is "simon-armitage-on-his-poem-the-manhunt".
- **Storm on the Island PDF:** the comparison table misquotes Exposure as "nothing happened". The poem's refrain is "But nothing happens".
- **War Photographer PDF:** quotes the line as "a priest preparing to impart (a) mass" in two places. The poem, and PMT's own annotated text, read "intone a Mass".
- **Poppies PDF:** the "Exposure" comparison block is copied from the Bayonet Charge PDF and discusses Hughes, not Weir.
- **Anthology currency:** PMT's AQA index now lists a "Worlds and Lives (NEW)" cluster. We need to confirm on AQA's site which anthology clusters apply to the student's exam year.
- **Copyright:** PMT reprints full in-copyright poems inside its PDFs. We should not copy this approach.

## 6. Things PMT has that our guide may lack
- A consistent "Brief Summary" plus bulleted "Synopsis" opener.
- A "The title" section and separate "The opening" / "The ending" analysis.
- Boxed term definitions next to the analysis (e.g. "MOTIF | ...").
- Comparisons shown as a two-column contrast table, then Similarities/Differences bullets with a quote from each poem.
- For unseen poetry, a 5-part paragraph checklist (quotes, analysis, reader effect, structure, alternative interpretations), the "Content and Ideas / Structure and Form / Language" planning triad, and a device glossary with a one-line example for each device.

Gaps on PMT that we could fill: Q26 technique, key-quote banks, an explicit theme-to-poem map, AO mapping per section, and model paragraphs.
