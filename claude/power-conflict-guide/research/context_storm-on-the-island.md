## 5. Form

| Fact | Source + exact sentence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Blank verse = unrhymed iambic pentameter | B-BV: "blank verse, unrhymed iambic pentameter, the preeminent dramatic and narrative verse form in English". POETS-BV: "Blank verse refers to poetry that does not rhyme but follows a regular meter, most commonly iambic pentameter." | SAFE |
| Blank verse can sound like speech | B-BV: Shakespeare's verse is "supple, approximating the rhythms of speech"; "The extreme flexibility of blank verse can be seen in its range from the high tragedy of Shakespeare to the low-keyed, conversational tone of Robert Frost in A Masque of Reason (1945)." | SAFE (one source, but definitional) |
| Storm is blank verse | Our check: 19 unrhymed lines, mostly ten syllables (line 1: "We are prepared: we build our houses squat," = 10, iambic). Lines vary. | SAFE (our check) |
| Dramatic monologue definition | B-DM: "A poem written in the form of a speech of an individual character; it compresses into a single vivid scene a narrative sense of the speaker's history and psychological insight into his character." | SAFE (definition) |
| Storm "is like" a dramatic monologue | PMT calls it one; the speaker is a "we" talking to a "you" ("as you see", "you know what I mean", "You might think"). Strictly the speaker is a community voice, not a named character. | USE WITH CARE: say "reads like a dramatic monologue" |
