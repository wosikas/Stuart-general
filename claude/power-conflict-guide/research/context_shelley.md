## 7. PMT Ozymandias notes: checked claim by claim

Source: pmt_dl/ozymandias.txt (PMT Education, CC BY-NC-ND).

### PMT errors (do NOT use)
1. **"Percy Shelley (1772-1822)" is wrong.** He was born in 1792 (B-PBS, POETS; see 1.1).
2. **"He got expelled from Oxford University for publishing an atheist pamphlet and delivering it to religious figures."** This is imprecise. He was expelled for refusing to say whether he wrote *The Necessity of Atheism* (B-PBS, UNIV, BOD; see 1.4). "Delivering it to religious figures" was not found on any source opened.
3. **"At 19 he got married after running off from his first wife who killed herself."** This is garbled. At 19 he eloped **with** Harriet, his first wife. He left her for Mary in 1814. Harriet drowned herself in late 1816, and he married Mary in December 1816 (1.5–1.7).
4. **"set to have ... a government role from his grandfather"** is unverified. Britannica says he was heir to his grandfather's estates and that his **father** was a Whig MP (1.2).
5. **The Romantic Era paragraph puts the Enlightenment where Romanticism should be.** It says "During the enlightenment, writers would center their work on the beauty of nature ... the sublime". Romanticism was "a reaction against the Enlightenment and against 18th-century rationalism" (B-ROM; see 3.2). Love of nature and the sublime is Romantic, not Enlightenment.
6. **"Ramesses II ... from 1279-1239 BC"** has the wrong end date. Britannica gives 1279–1213 BC (6.2).
7. **"believed to be the pharaoh involved in Moses' exodus. He was later defeated by the 12 tribes of Israel"** is NOT supported. Britannica: "Ramses II has received a bad rap on some fronts, however, often being conflated with the tyrannical pharaoh from the Book of Exodus, but historical and archaeological evidence does not support this." (B-RAM.) No source supports any defeat by the tribes of Israel. Delete it.
8. **"used the throne name 'Ozymandias'"** is inaccurate. "Ozymandias" is the **Greek** name for Ramesses II (B-OZ, RPO, B-Q).
9. **"remembered for his defeat"** is unsupported. Kadesh is his "most important campaign" (B-RAM). Britannica's own account calls it a stalemate (fetch-tool summary), not a defeat.
10. **"his statue had just been discovered at Shelley's time of writing"** is misleading. It had been known since Napoleon's expedition (c.1798), when the French tried to move it. Belzoni removed it in 1816, and it was on its way to London (4.2, 6.4).
11. **George III in 1818:** PMT writes as if George III was actively ruling and waging wars when the poem was written. In fact he had been declared incurably insane in 1811, and the Prince Regent ruled from 1811 (4.3). "Reigned longer than any king before" was not verified.
12. **"Shelley uses Ozymandias as an allegory for King George III"** is stated as fact. No source opened says so (Britannica's Ozymandias article does not mention George III). It is an interpretation: say "could be read as…". Shelley's documented attack on George III is *England in 1819*, written after Ozymandias (4.9).
13. **Rhyme scheme:** "closer to ABAB CDCD EFEF GG" is wrong. RPO gives "ababacdcedefef", with no final couplet (RPO-OZ; matches strand1).
14. **"Sonnets are traditionally love poems ... making this an object of love"** is the exact argument AQA's June 2023 examiner report criticised as unhelpful ("he uses a sonnet because sonnets are love poems which shows Ozymandias loves himself"; see exam_materials.md). Avoid it.
15. **Unverified, so do not use:** "inspired by the French Revolution" (not found for Shelley specifically); "vegetarian" (only indirectly supported: *Queen Mab* attacks "the eating of meat", B-PBS); "13 colonies rebelled … remembered for oppression and tyranny" (Britannica says only that Britain "lost its American colonies"). Minor: PMT spells the name "Oxymandias" once and "rein" for "reign".

### PMT points that ARE verified (now in this file)
Radical, anti-monarchy, anti-religion (2.1, 2.2); expelled from Oxford over an atheist pamphlet (1.4, with the correction above); wealthy family (1.2); second-generation Romantic (3.3); Horace Smith challenge (5.4); the statue came from the Ramesseum (6.4); Ramesses' military campaigns (6.3); his opposition to war (2.4, 2.8, partly).

### Useful things PMT lacks (now found)
The Examiner's politics and the Hunts' imprisonment (4.4, 5.2); the pseudonym Glirastes (5.1); the Regency (4.3); Napoleon's hole in the statue (4.2); the poem appearing before the statue arrived (6.5); the Diodorus inscription (6.6); "unacknowledged legislators" (2.6); the Exodus myth corrected (7.7).

## Gaps and conflicts
- **GAP:** the exact date of composition (late 1817?) was not found on any opened page.
- **GAP:** no second publisher (other than Britannica) for the Romanticism definition, or for "rejected monarchy, religion, war, and commerce". Poetry Foundation and the British Library were blocked.
- **GAP:** post-1815 unrest such as Spa Fields (1816) and Pentrich (1817): no source opened. The National Archives pages returned 410.
- **GAP:** Shelley's burial in Rome (the Non-Catholic Cemetery) appeared only in search snippets and was not opened. Not needed for AO3.
- **GAP:** when the British Museum formally acquired the bust (1818 or 1821?). It was only in a search snippet. Say "arrived in London in 1818".
- **CONFLICT:** Ramesses' dates: 1279–1213 BC (Britannica, SOAS; BM's c.1279 plus 67 years) against 1304–1237 BC (RPO). Use "13th century BC".
- **CONFLICT:** Peterloo was 16 Aug 1819 (Britannica ×2), but the RPO note was returned as "August 19, 1819". Use 16 August.
