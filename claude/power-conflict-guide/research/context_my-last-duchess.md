## 5. Victorian attitudes to women and marriage (use carefully: the poem is SET in the 1500s)

| # | Fact | Source | Status | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Under English common law ("coverture") a wife's legal identity merged into her husband's. | B-COV: "Anglo-American common-law concept… that dictated a woman's subordinate legal status during marriage"; "her legal existence as an individual was suspended under 'marital unity,' a legal fiction in which the husband and wife were considered a single entity: the husband." | SAFE as a legal fact (Britannica; BHAM agrees on property) | Women as possessions |
| 5.2 | A wife's personal property passed to her husband on marriage, until the Married Women's Property Acts of 1870 and 1882. | BHAM: "Any personal property a woman owned, either through wages, investment, or inheritance, belonged to her husband upon marriage." | 5.3 | Divorce before 1858 needed a private Act of Parliament; 320 such Acts 1700–1857, only four for women. | TNA: "In order to remarry within the lifetime of a spouse, a full divorce by Act of Parliament was required, with proof of adultery or life-threatening cruelty." ## 7. PMT My Last Duchess notes: checked
Errors: Browning married Elizabeth Barrett in 1846, not 1845 (B-EBB, B-RB). "Spent most of life in Italy" overstated (1846–61 only). "Italian resistance" should be Renaissance. "Fra is the prefix for a monk": RPO says "a member of religious orders" (friar) — close enough but say "friar/religious brother". Victorian claims (Industrial Revolution/suffrage starting, women "incapable of rational thought") unverified — not used. "Alfronzo/Ferraro" typos. PMT says he "went on to have other wives later" — confirmed (three marriages, B-ALF).
Verified from PMT: Alfonso II as model; suspicious death (RPO); dramatic monologue; iambic pentameter (EBSCO); enjambment (EBSCO); curtain and Neptune as symbols (AQA ER23 praises discussion of both).
PMT lacks (now found): Browning's own "put to death… convent" remark (RPO, PMLA); the Count of Tyrol (RPO); Medici merchant origins vs ancient Este (RPO); NCMA portrait; National Archives divorce figures; AQA's "grieving" warning.
