## 7. PMT leads checked
- "lived in Northern Ireland during the troubles in the 1980's" and "has two sons": NOT found on any allowed source. GAP; do not use.
- "Poppies … became a symbol of remembrance in 1921": consistent with Britannica/RBL (paper poppies sold from 1921).
- "armistice Sunday also became a way to remember World War Two": consistent with Britannica (Remembrance Sunday honours both World Wars).
- "the son participating in armistice Sunday and then being killed in war himself": OVERCLAIM. The poem never says he died; keep it open.
- PMT's "Exposure" comparison is about Bayonet Charge (known error).

## Gaps and conflicts
- Guardian original blocked: the evidence that Poppies was in Exit Wounds = Pearson MS 2020 + Oak National Academy (both secondary, but independent). IWM record also blocked.
- No Weir statement/interview on an allowed source. The "woman's perspective" quote and "two teenage sons" exist only on revision/school sites.
- Northern Ireland years: GAP.
- Date: Pearson's exam booklet "2005" vs 2009 (Pearson MS, Oak, WOA). Use 2009.
- Stanza count in verify4 (6/12/11/6) is wrong; anth.txt gives 6/11/12/6. Oak's "two sestets either side of two longer stanzas" agrees with both but the middle order is ours.
- 2009 "bloodiest year" (109 deaths) and Helmand 2006: NAM only.
- How the Sunday is fixed: Britannica "second Sunday" vs Pearson "Sunday closest to 11 November".
