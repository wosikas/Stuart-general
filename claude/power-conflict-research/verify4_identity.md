## USE WITH CARE

1. **[AQA-PAPER-K] series label.** The footer does read "IB/G/Jun23/8702/2R", and the paper code is 8702/2**R**, not 8702/2. AQA's own June 2023 8702/2 paper (filestore, footer "IB/M/Jun23/E9") sets Q26 on **My Last Duchess**, not Kamikaze. So the Kamikaze paper is *not* the standard June 2023 paper, and PMT's "June 2024" label is plausible. Cite it as "AQA 8702/2R (PMT: June 2024)". Do not call it June 2023.
2. **Poppies heading, "first published 2009 in the Guardian 'Exit Wounds' commission".** [WOA] confirms the Guardian commission (25 July 2009) but names no poets. [PEARSON-MS20] puts Poppies in *Exit Wounds*, "published in 2009". That Poppies itself appeared in the Guardian is therefore inferred from two sources and is SINGLE SOURCE for the poem. The Guardian page is blocked.
3. **Poppies context 6 (Armistice Sunday).** The quote is verbatim, but it is SINGLE SOURCE, and Pearson's own wording loosely equates "Armistice Sunday" with "Remembrance Day". Keep the student's phrasing to "Remembrance/Armistice Sunday, the Sunday nearest 11 November", as Pearson says.
4. **Emigrée context 2.** The [OL-SKINS] phrase is verbatim, in full: "She draws on a wide variety of characters and voices to dramatise the realities of suffering and persecution, or to write direct, honest accounts of love, separation, death and displacement." However, the strand's gloss "often in Eastern Europe/Russia" is **not on that page**, so it is an overclaim. Either drop that gloss or attribute the Eastern Europe interest to Rumens herself, via [PA-RUMENS].
5. **Emigrée context 3 inference.** "She is not herself an émigrée, so the poem is a voice/persona" is the researcher's inference. The source gives only her birthplace. Phrase it as: Rumens was born in London, so the speaker is probably an imagined voice.
6. **Emigrée date 1993.** This is the date of the collection, and *Thinking of Skins* is "new & selected", so the poem's first publication could be earlier. Say "collected in *Thinking of Skins* (1993)".
7. **Kamikaze "written in 2013".** This is SINGLE SOURCE [WJEC-K]. The strand's Gap 6 comment calling it the "collection date" is an inference.
8. **Kamikaze Pearl Harbor note.** The advice to drop the WJEC Pearl Harbour claim is right. However, [BRIT-K] has no Pearl Harbor mention and no start date. It only says the practice was "most prevalent from the Battle of Leyte Gulf, October 1944". The "conflict" is therefore indirect: Britannica does not explicitly contradict WJEC. Keep the advice and soften the wording to "not supported by Britannica".
9. **Weir 1963 birth year.** [WEM-WEIR] has it verbatim. The [PA-WEIR] fetch reported "born in 1963" but only in a summary, so treat the Poetry Archive as corroboration only.

## DROP / FIX

1. **The Emigrée first line: FIX to AQA wording.** [AQA-ANTH] prints "**There once was a country…**" (Pearson prints "There was once a country…"). AQA's line 3 also differs: "for it seems I never saw it in **that** November" (Pearson: "in November"). The student should learn the AQA versions. Gap 4 is now resolved in favour of "There once was". Update the Methods bullet ("Opening 'There was once a country…'") to match.
2. **The Emigrée title/poet spelling (note, not an error in the strand).** [AQA-ANTH] heads the poem "The Émigree" and prints the poet as "CAROLE RUMENS", which is an AQA typo. The AQA exam paper list uses "The Emigrée" / "Carol Rumens". Tell the student to use "The Emigrée" and "Carol Rumens".
3. **Poppies Gap 3: FIX.** [AQA-ANTH] line 26 reads "and this is where it **has led** me," while Pearson reads "where it led me,". Use the AQA wording.
4. **Poppies "What happens": minor FIX.** The strand has her following a dove "up to the war memorial in the churchyard". The poem has her "skirting the church yard walls" and then tracing the memorial "On reaching the top of the hill". It does not put the memorial in the churchyard. Corrected wording: she follows a dove past the churchyard walls and up the hill to the war memorial.

## Tissue (quotations listed in strand1_power.md)

It is credited "Imtiaz Dharker ‘Tissue’ reprinted with permission of Bloodaxe Books, on behalf of the author." This is AQA's own anthology text, so it is the best possible check.

**Form claims that can now be verified** (strand1 listed them as a GAP):
- There are 10 stanzas: nine quatrains plus a one-line final stanza, 37 lines in all. One stanza break falls at a page break ("grand design / with living tissue"). It is consistent with the 4-line pattern, but confirm it in the printed anthology.
- There is no regular rhyme scheme.
- First-person speaker: "I might / feel their drift".
- Stanza 4 is "If buildings were paper, I might / feel their drift". It is **missing from the search-snippet versions** of the poem circulating online. This is a further reason to quote only from the AQA text.
- The strand1 "What happens" summary (old books/Koran records, maps, receipts, the architect, ending in "living tissue") matches the text. Note that the poem's "receipts" are "Fine slips from grocery shops", printed with a capital F in the AQA text.

All six Tissue quotations can be changed from UNVERIFIED to VERIFIED against the AQA anthology.
