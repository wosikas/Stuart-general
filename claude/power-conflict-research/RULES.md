# Research rules (binding for every agent)

Purpose: a revision guide for a Year 11 student (AQA GCSE English Literature 8702, Paper 2 Section B, Q26: compare the printed Power and Conflict poem with one other from memory; 30 marks: AO1 12, AO2 12, AO3 6). He is new to the poems.

## Accuracy
- No facts from memory. Every fact, date, context claim and critical reading must come from a page you OPENED in this session (WebFetch). Record the URL and the exact sentence you relied on (copy it verbatim).
- A search snippet is not a source. Open the page.
- If two reliable sources disagree, record both. A fact backed by only one source = mark "SINGLE SOURCE".
- If you cannot find a source for something, write "GAP: ..." — a gap is a finding, not a failure. Never fill it from memory.
- Quotation marks only around verbatim text. Your own wording is never in quotes.

## Allowed sources (in order of preference)
AQA filestore (filestore.aqa.org.uk) and aqa.org.uk; PMT (pmt.physicsandmathstutor.com — use WebFetch, not curl) for past papers; British Library Discovering Literature (bl.uk); Poetry Foundation; Poetry Archive (poetryarchive.org); poets' and publishers' official pages; author interviews in reputable outlets (Guardian, BBC, etc.); Britannica; Imperial War Museums (iwm.org.uk); National Archives; National Army Museum; university pages; Royal Collection; British Museum.
- NOT allowed (ask first — so don't use): LitCharts, SparkNotes, Save My Exams, BBC Bitesize, Seneca, school, tutor or revision-company sites, Course Hero, essay sites. Wikipedia may be used only to find a primary source, never as the cited source.
- If a tool is blocked for a site (403 etc.), don't work around it with curl/python; note it and use another source.

## Quotations from poems
- Check every quotation word for word (including punctuation and capitals) against a published text you opened (Poetry Foundation, Poetry Archive, publisher, British Library, Project Gutenberg / Bartleby for old poems). Record the URL. If sources differ in wording, flag it.
- Copyright: poets still in copyright (Heaney, Hughes, Armitage, Weir, Duffy, Dharker, Rumens, Agard, Garland; Owen died 1918 so out of copyright) — quote short phrases only (max ~1 line / 10 words each) in your output. Never reproduce a whole stanza.
- If you cannot find the full text of an in-copyright poem online, quote only phrases you can verify on a reputable page (e.g. publisher, Poetry Archive, BL, a newspaper) and mark the rest "UNVERIFIED — check against anthology".

## Output
Write your results to the file path named in your brief, in markdown. Be complete but tight. End with a short "Gaps and conflicts" list.
