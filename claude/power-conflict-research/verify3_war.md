## SAFE (checked, correct)

**Remains (text from AQA-N20-QP, 30 numbered lines)**
- Quotations 1–8 are all word for word and punctuation for punctuation, including the dash after "life –" and the full stops in "probably armed, possibly not." and "End of story, except not really."
- The 5 "extra lines" are SAFE: "His blood-shadow stays on the street"; "he bursts again through the doors of the bank"; "And the drink and the drugs won't flush him out –"; "he's here in my head when I close my eyes"; "near to the knuckle, here and now".

- The plain-English summary is accurate.

## USE WITH CARE

1. **The Not Dead dates.** The official site says the film was shown in summer 2007 and the book was published Feb 5th, 2004 (an evident error). The bookseller says Pomona 2008. "Film 2007, book 2008" is the best reading, but the book year rests on the minor bookseller site only.
2. **Remains context 2 (Basra, a bank looter).** The detail "Basra" is single-source (a minor bookseller site). WJEC-R corroborates Iraq and a looter ("routine patrol in Iraq… armed looter"), so "Iraq" can be stated with two sources, but keep "Basra" marked single source.
3. **Guardsman Tromans / Iraq 2003 / PTSD, and Armitage's purpose.** WJEC-R only (an exam board, so acceptable, but single). **Exam advice from AQA's own examiners' report (AQA June 2022, recorded in exam_materials.md):** "paragraphs about, for example, Owen's war experiences, Hughes' father as a soldier or extraneous information about Guardsman Tromans were not useful if they meant the student lost sight of the poem." Tell the student to use Tromans and Hughes's father only briefly and tied to the poem.
4. **Sarah Crown Guardian review.** It is verbatim on the bookseller page, but it is a second-hand copy (the Guardian original was not opened). Fine to use with that label.
5. **Hughes's father at Gallipoli.** Single source (INFLIBNET university e-text). Same AQA caution as item 3.
6. **McCullin / Jones Griffiths inspiration for War Photographer, and the "almost 3 million" Cambodia figure.** WJEC-WP only. Keep marked single source, and treat the death toll as an estimate.
7. **UVic course pages (Tennyson Examiner / Maud / pamphlets).** These are student/course pages, so secondary. Note that the companion page attributes the Maud facts to "(Houston 357)", a secondary citation. The pamphlet story is now on both UVic pages, but they are the same site, so it is still effectively single source.
8. **Casualty figures for the charge.** All three sources were re-verified and they genuinely differ. Use NAM's "about 260 of 673 killed or wounded", as the strand advises.
9. **War Photographer text variants.** AQA has "darkroom", "black-and-white" and "pick out five or six". SPL has "dark room" and "black and white". **New variant:** WJEC-WP's quote list reads "from which his editor will pick five or six" (no "out"), and its notes use "dark room". Use the AQA wording and check against the anthology.
10. **Bayonet Charge plain English: "A First World War soldier (implied by the 'khaki', rifle and bayonet)".** The poem never names the war, and khaki, rifles and bayonets were used in many wars. WWI is the usual reading (Hughes's father; "King"), but no allowed source opened says the poem is set in WWI. Better wording: "a soldier, usually read as First World War, though the poem never says".
11. **War Photographer plain English: "he flies off to the next war".** The text says only "From the aeroplane he stares impassively at where / he earns his living". Whether he is flying out to a war zone or looking down on England is open. Better wording: "on a plane, he looks down impassively at where he earns his living".
12. **Tennyson metre "dactylic dimeter" and the Bible echoes** (Psalm 23 "valley of the shadow of death"; Isaiah "All flesh is grass"). The strand already flags these as the writer's own, unsourced. I tried to confirm the Bible verses on Project Gutenberg's KJV, but the page is too long to reach Psalms/Isaiah in one fetch. Still unverified from an opened page, so keep them as "possible echo".
13. **Remains, "This fits its origin in a real soldier's spoken testimony".** This is supported in general by Armitage's site (poems "focus on the testimonies of veterans"). Note, though, that the official site lists Gulf, Bosnia and Malayan veterans and does not mention Iraq. The Iraq link comes from WJEC and the bookseller.
14. **Poetry Archive Tennyson wording.** The page reads "being made Poet Laureate in 1850 following the death of Wordsworth". The strand's quotation begins "he was made Poet Laureate…", which is not verbatim. The fact is fine, but fix the quotation marks (see DROP/FIX 4).

## DROP / FIX

1. **Remains quotation 4, "A switch to the present tense".** This is a misreading. The whole poem is narrated in the present tense from line 1 ("we get sent out", "one of them legs it", "all three of us open fire"), so there is no switch at line 9. **Fix:** "The repeated 'I see' makes the narration intensely visual and first-person singular: he is reliving the shooting as if it is happening in front of him." The point about "round … rips" alliteration stays. Similarly, in Methods "Present tense and repetition" the present tense is right, but it is the poem's narrative tense throughout, not a sudden device.
2. **War Photographer Methods, rhyme: "later stanzas follow the same pattern with some half-rhymes (then / again)".** Wrong as stated. "again" is mid-line (l.9 "Home again"), not a line end. Checked against AQA-J19-QP, the line-end words are:
   - stanza 1: alone / rows / glows / he / Mass / grass, which is ABBCDD.
   - stanza 2: trays / then / now / dispel / feet / heat. Lines 2–3 do not rhyme; only the final couplet (feet / heat) does.
   - stanza 3: features / eyes / cries / approval / must / dust, which is ABBCDD.
   - stanza 4: black-and-white / six / prick / beers / where / care, which is ABBCDD with the half-rhyme six / prick.

   **Fix:** "Stanzas 1, 3 and 4 follow ABBCDD (stanza 4 with a half-rhyme, six / prick); stanza 2 breaks the pattern in lines 2–3 (then / now), just where he remembers the war zone. Every stanza ends in a rhyming couplet." WJEC's "consistent rhyme scheme (ABBCDD)" is therefore a slight overclaim. Quote it, but don't repeat it as fact.
3. **Tennyson heading: "(written 2 Dec 1854; first published in *The Examiner*, 9 Dec 1854)".** The heading states the 2 Dec date as fact, but the strand's own Context 4 and Gaps 4 say it is UNVERIFIED (search snippet only). **Fix:** remove "written 2 Dec 1854", or mark it "UNVERIFIED". 9 Dec 1854 in the Examiner is verified (UVic).
4. **Tennyson Context 3 quotation.** Change "he was made Poet Laureate in 1850 following the death of Wordsworth" to the verbatim "being made Poet Laureate in 1850 following the death of Wordsworth".
5. **Bayonet Charge Context 4 / Gaps 5, "Bayonet Charge is not named".** This needs correcting. The INFLIBNET page does name it elsewhere: "Others like _Bayonet charge, Six Young Men_ and _The Martyrdom of Bishop Farrar_ present human violence." It does not say which volume the poem is in. **The claim that Bayonet Charge is in *The Hawk in the Rain* (1957) is still not confirmed by any allowed page I could open** (search results point only to Wikipedia, LitCharts and school/revision sites, which are not allowed). Keep it as a GAP, and remove "1957" from the heading or mark it "(check)".
