## USE WITH CARE

1. **York Notes interview (Tissue).** The quotations are accurate, but York Notes is a study-guide publisher and RULES.md does not allow it. The strand already flags this for caller approval. Everything in the Tissue Big ideas, Methods and Context sections that relies on Dharker's words, and the Ozymandias–Tissue "author link", depends on that approval.
2. **Tissue quotations 1–6 and all form claims** remain UNVERIFIED (the poet's page is 404; no allowed text was found). They are correctly labelled.
   - Reading of quotation 4 ("a structure / never meant to last"): the strand glosses it as buildings and power being temporary, "compare Ozymandias". Check the context in the anthology. The phrase appears to be part of the speaker's hopeful wish to build something deliberately impermanent, not a lament that power fades, so the parallel with Ozymandias should be worded as a contrast of attitude, not a straight match.
3. **London metre ("iambic tetrameter").** The Cash quotation is verbatim, but it is a single source. In the AQA text several lines in stanzas 3–4 have seven syllables and begin on a stress ("How the chimney-sweeper's cry", "And the hapless soldier's sigh"). Say "mostly four-beat, iambic tetrameter" rather than claiming strict regularity.
4. **London Cash material (draft revisions, Chimney Sweepers Act 1834, the Paine link).** The quotations are verbatim, but all of it rests on one teacher's article (already marked SINGLE SOURCE).
   - The BL page independently refers to Blake's "depiction of the 'dirty' streets of London", which is mild indirect support for the "dirty" draft reading but does not confirm the revision.
   - Cash's "no effective legislation" before 1834 is his judgement, not a historian's.
5. **The bracket inside the Cash quotation (London metre).** The strand inserts "['every']" inside quotation marks. Cash's text reads "the first syllable of the word"; the bracket is an editorial insertion, so keep the square brackets visible or move the gloss outside the quote.
6. **Single-source items** confirmed as single-source:
   - the British Museum 1818 bust (Britannica only);
   - the Diodorus inscription wording (RPO);
   - the Alfonso II / Lucrezia details (RPO);
   - Agard's 1949 birth year (Poetry Archive);
   - "composed… summer of 1792" (BL China site).
7. **The "first published" date for Checking Out Me History in the section heading.** The Poetry Archive credit says only "From Half-Caste and Other Poems (Hodder Children's, 2004)". It does not say that is where the poem was first published. Reword as "text as printed in *Half-Caste and Other Poems* (2004)".
8. **Checking Out Me History, no punctuation.** Confirmed as displayed on the Poetry Archive, but the fetch tool may strip formatting, so still check the anthology.
9. **The MLD envoy reading.** "The visitor is in fact arranging the Duke's next marriage" is a fair inference from ll.48–53 ("The Count your master's…", "his fair daughter's self… is my object"). It is supported by the text but not by a cited source; fine as interpretation.
10. **The title spelling "Checkin'" vs "Checking".** Confirmed that AQA uses both. Follow the anthology.

## DROP / FIX

1. **Ozymandias, "Text checked against: poets.org … matches the AQA wording except … shatter'd/stamp'd/mock'd".** This is an overclaim. poets.org also differs in punctuation and capitals: l.4 "…visage lies, whose frown," (comma); l.5 "And wrinkled lip, and sneer of cold command," (extra commas); l.8 "The hand that mocked them, and the heart that fed:" (comma and colon; AQA has no comma and ends ";"); l.11 "Look on my works, ye Mighty, and despair!'" (capital M; AQA "ye mighty"). FIX: say poets.org differs in spelling and in several commas and capitals; the quotations themselves (which follow AQA) are unaffected.
2. **Checking Out Me History, Methods ("Irony and tone"): "Old King Cole".** The Poetry Archive text reads "ole King Cole". FIX: quote it as "ole King Cole" (or drop the quotation marks).
3. **Checking Out Me History, Context 3 (Toussaint): "transformed a large slave revolt…".** Not verbatim. Britannica reads: "He played a central role in transforming a large slave revolt in the French colony of Saint-Domingue on the island of Hispaniola (later Haiti) into a political and military struggle that abolished slavery and established a self-governing society under nominal French authority." The opening sentence is "Toussaint Louverture was a leader of the Haitian independence movement during the French Revolutionary era (1787–99)" (lower-case "a"). FIX: replace with that wording. The fact is correct.
4. **Checking Out Me History, Methods (dialect): "The Poetry Archive poet page (fetch summary) describes him making creative use of dialect as resistance against establishment norms."** No such sentence was found on https://poetryarchive.org/poet/john-agard/ (the page quotes "making de Queen's English accessory to my offence", from another poem). DROP the attribution. Keep the point as the student's own observation from the poem's language.
5. **Tissue, Context 1: themes quoted as "childhood, exile, journeying, home, displacement, religious strife and terror, and grief".** Bloodaxe's actual wording is "Her main themes are drawn from a life of transitions: childhood, exile, journeying, home, displacement, religious strife and terror, and latterly, grief." The quotation omits "latterly," inside quote marks. FIX the quote. Also note this is the publisher's description of Dharker's themes generally, on the book page; it is not a description of Tissue.
