## 3. Readings of the poem (critics)

| Point | Source + exact sentence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Subject: role reversal over time | EA: "In this poem, Heaney's interest is in the ironic effects of time upon the relationship between his father Patrick and himself: in particular, his aim is to illustrate the dramatic reversal of roles between his father and himself that the passage of time has brought about." | USE WITH CARE (one critic; matches AQA MS "change in child's perspective") |
| Double meaning of "follower" | EA: "Out of this situation arises the double meaning of the title: for his son Seamus' ambition to be a metaphorical 'follower' in his father's footsteps, his awe-struck pursuit of his father up the ploughed land supplies a literal context." | USE WITH CARE (one critic) |
| Shakespeare "second childishness" | EA epigraph: "Last scene of all .... is second childishness" – Shakespeare: As You Like It | USE WITH CARE (critic's link) |
| Ending: impatience? | EA: "we feel that Heaney does not have the patience with his 'stumbling' father that his father had with his stumbling son". | USE WITH CARE (one reading; a second reading: the father "will not go away" from his memory/as an influence) |
| Ending: tradition behind him | PFS (paraphrase via fetch tool, partly quoted): "His father's tradition is dead...but yet it stands behind Heaney in his encounter with modernity." | USE WITH CARE (one source; tool excerpt with ellipsis) |
| EA dates the memory to the mid-1940s | EA: "a memoir of his father in the mid-1940s". PFS: "rural Ulster in the early 1940s" (about his culture, not the poem). | USE WITH CARE (critic's inference) |

## 4. Form

| Fact | Source + exact sentence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Six quatrains, abab | PFS: "'Follower' consists of six four-line stanzas, or quatrains. Each stanza follows an abab rhyme scheme." Our check of anth.txt: 24 lines, 6 × 4. | SAFE |
| Many half-rhymes | Our check: plough/furrow, strung/tongue, wing/breaking, sock/pluck, eye/exactly, wake/back, plough/follow, falling/stumbling are half-rhymes; full rhymes round/ground, sod/plod, arm/farm, today/away. ## 5. Horse-ploughing (general context, not about Heaney's farm specifically)

| Fact | Source + exact sentence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Plough parts | NIA: "The Coulter- a knife which makes a vertical cut in the soil. The share- makes a horizontal cut. The Mouldboard or breast gently turns over the slice of soil cut by the coulter and share." | USE WITH CARE (one heritage source) |
| "Sock" = ploughshare (Irish dialect) | PFS: "The pointed blade attached to the front of the moldboard that does the cutting is called a ploughshare, although Heaney makes a point of referring to it by an Irish dialectical word that is only a homonym to the English word 'sock'". | USE WITH CARE (one source) |

| Ploughing horses were skilled; turning needed little guidance | NIA: "Well trained horses knew how to pull a plough steadily and smoothly; they knew how to pull out and turn with effortless motion requiring little or no guidance from the ploughman." | USE WITH CARE (one source) |

## Gaps and conflicts
