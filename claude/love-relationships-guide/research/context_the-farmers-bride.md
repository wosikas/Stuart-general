## 6. AQA on this poem (from lr/exam_materials_lr.md)
- Printed Nov 2021 (8702/1P Q01): "Compare how poets present strong feelings in romantic relationships in ‘The Farmer’s Bride’ and in one other poem from ‘Love and relationships’."
- ER-N21: "showing a good understanding of the famer’s [sic] attitude as revealed in the first person narration. Most comments were in response to the first thirty lines of the poem, however better answers were able to recognise and comment on the mounting tension and frustration of the last verse. Most students were able to address AO2 in some way, especially the similes in the poem and their effect on the reader, [‘like a mouse’, ‘shy as a leveret’]. Although, the opportunity to address gender issues as a way to access AO3 was sadly often missed." Effective partners: "Porphyria’s Lover [students engaged with this, especially well alongside The Farmer’s Bride], Sonnet 29 – I think of thee and When We Two Parted." Not appropriate: Mother any distance, Walking Away.
- MS-N21 partners: Porphyria's Lover, Neutral Tones, Love's Philosophy, Sonnet 29, Singh Song!, Winter Swans. Also named in MS17, MS18 ("the more cynical presentation of love"), MS19 ("the change in feelings in ‘The Farmer’s Bride’"), MS-J22 (nature imagery), MS24 ("negative effects of powerful feelings").
- ER-J22: with Porphyria's Lover, "especially seemed to engage the students, perhaps because of their narrative nature".
