## 5. Form (our count from anth.txt + Britannica definitions)

- 27 lines, one single unbroken stanza. No rhyme scheme, no regular metre: free verse. B-FV: "poetry organized to the cadences of speech and image patterns rather than according to a regular metrical scheme." Applying it = our check (SAFE).
- First person ("I"), present tense ("I decide", "I discover", "I rest"). Our check.
- Heavy enjambment: e.g. 5–6 "I change / direction", 10 "like warm ice" carries on from 9, 22–23 bracket runs across line. B-ENJ: "the continuation of the sense of a phrase beyond the end of a line of verse."
- Journey shape: feet (brogues, l.2) → trousers → shirt/belt → hand → arm → shoulder (l.13) → neck → mouth → cheek → eyes → forehead → hair → "summit" (l.23) → lies down (l.24) → heart (l.27). Our check.
- Only one full stop in the middle runs on long sentences; full stops at lines 1, 4, 7, 12, 17, 20, 27. Our check.
- Mountaineering vocabulary: free (climbing without ropes), rope or net, scramble, grip, overhanging, traverse, purchase, ridge, screed (sic in anthology; scree = loose stones on a mountainside), altitude, summit, gasping for breath. Our check. "screed" spelling: anth.txt prints "screed cheek".
