## 4. Literary background and form

| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Pathetic fallacy = "poetic practice of attributing human emotion or responses to nature, inanimate objects, or animals"; term coined by John Ruskin in *Modern Painters* (1843–60). | B-PF | USE WITH CARE (one source; definition only) |
| 4.2 | A critic (Hoffpauir, cited on VW) reads the poem's nature as "essentially neutral, unsympathetic to human goings on". Banerjee (VW) says "the poet expresses the pervading sadness of life — human as well as natural". | VW | USE WITH CARE (one page) — useful debate: is nature reflecting him, or just indifferent? |
| 4.3 | Four quatrains (4-line stanzas), 16 lines; rhyme ABBA in every stanza (day/grey, God/sod; rove/love [half-rhyme], ago/fro; thing/a-wing, die/thereby; deceives/leaves, me/tree). Line 4 of each stanza is indented and shorter. | Our count from anth.txt | SAFE (own count) |
| 4.4 | Circular structure: last stanza repeats the face, sun, tree and pond (with leaves) from stanza 1. | Our count | SAFE (own count); meaning = our reading |
| 4.5 | Direct address to "you" ("Your eyes", "your mouth", "Your face"). AQA MS24 names "direct address in ‘Neutral Tones’ compared with ‘Winter Swans’ or ‘Love’s Philosophy’". | anth.txt; AQA MS24 | SAFE |
| 4.6 | Metre: PMT says "tetrameter" — not verified; avoid naming the metre. | — | GAP |

## 5. AQA material for this poem (from lr/exam_materials_lr.md)
- Printed June 2024 (8702/2R): "Compare how poets present powerful feelings about love in ‘Neutral Tones’ and in one other poem from ‘Love and relationships’." MS24 suggested partners: Winter Swans, Love’s Philosophy, Porphyria’s Lover, The Farmer’s Bride, Singh Song!, Sonnet 29.
- MS24 AO1: "possible comparison between bleak and more positive views of love in ‘Winter Swans’…"; "any valid treatment of different types of powerful feelings, eg … hope for the future in ‘Winter Swans’"; "comparison of negative effects of powerful feelings, eg ‘Porphyria’s Lover’…". AO2: "direct address in ‘Neutral Tones’"; "possible views of imagery of life/death to present powerful feelings". AO3: "ideas about control, or repression, or freedom"; "different types of strong feelings and their effects on relationships and/or on the self".
- Named as a partner: MS18 ("compared with ‘When We Two Parted’, or ‘Neutral Tones’"; "the ending of a relationship in ‘Neutral Tones’"), MS-N21 ("negative effects of strong feelings, eg ‘Porphyria’s Lover’ or ‘Neutral Tones’"; "sadness in ‘Neutral Tones’"), MS-J22 ("negative powerful effects of love, such as ‘Neutral Tones’ or ‘Porphyria’s Lover’"; "first-person speaker … ‘Neutral Tones’"). ER-J22: among the most popular partners; "All of these worked well".
