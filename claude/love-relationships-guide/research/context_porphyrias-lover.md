## 3. The name "Porphyria" and the PMT disease claim (WRONG)
| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | The medical term "porphyria" was coined in 1889, 53 years after the poem. So Browning cannot have named her after the disease. | VW-W: "the term _was not coined until 1889_, when Dr Barend Stovkis coined the term for the blood disorder after the Greek 'porphyrus' (purple)." "Robert Browning would not — could not — have called the disease 'porphyria' unless he could see 53 years into the future." The Victorian Web lists this as a correction to VW-B's disease reading. | USE WITH CARE (student author, single source) but it refutes PMT and VW-B; GALE also mentions the disease link uncritically. **Do not use the disease reading.** |
| 3.2 | The name comes from Greek for purple; purple is linked with royalty, so the name may hint at high status. | VW-W: "purple is commonly associated with royalty". GALE also lists "porphyry" (a stone) among meanings. | USE WITH CARE (one source for the royalty link; our reading) |

## 4. Form
| # | Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | 60 lines; rhyme ababb; twelve hidden five-line units, printed as one block. | RPO: "Notice how the twelve, five-line stanzas are hidden by the poem's diction and visual appearance." RPO: ababb rhyme. GALE: "it is composed of sixty lines of verse divided into twelve sets of five lines each which rhyme _ababb_". EBSCO: "ababb rhyme scheme, a pattern which continues through the poem's twelve five-line divisions". anth.txt: 60 lines, one block. | SAFE |
| 4.2 | Metre: iambic tetrameter (four beats). | EBSCO: "'Porphyria's Lover' is a sixty-line poem of irregular iambic tetrameter with an ababb rhyme scheme". **CONFLICT:** GALE says "Most of the poem is written in an uncomplicated iambic pentameter". Our count: line 1 "The rain set early in to-night" = 8 syllables, 4 beats, which is tetrameter. | USE WITH CARE: tetrameter (EBSCO + our count); GALE is wrong on this point |
| 4.3 | Dramatic monologue (definition reused from BAYLOR/Abrams). Nuance: EBSCO says it might be "more accurately termed a soliloquy or an inner monologue than a dramatic monologue, since it identifies no specific auditor." | BAYLOR; GALE; EBSCO | SAFE (with EBSCO's nuance) |
| 4.4 | First-person narration cannot be trusted: "First-person narration provides no sure way of authenticating the speaker's statements." | VW-L (Landow) | SAFE as a method point (single but authoritative academic) |
| 4.5 | AQA points students to "the use of first person to present a particular view of reality/self, eg with 'Porphyria's Lover'" and to its "narrative/events". | MS-N21 AO2 | SAFE (AQA verbatim) |

| # | Reading | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|

## 7. PMT notes: checked
- WRONG: the porphyria-disease claim ("discovered a few years before the poem was written") — term coined 1889 (VW-W). Not used.
- Unverified/not used: "scandals… Browning has to be extremely transgressive… to shock a numbed listener" (GALE has a related point about scandal sheets — used with care); "soiled" = cheating; "burning" = devil; class of Porphyria (only VW-W purple/royalty, Landow "pride").
- Verified from PMT: dramatic monologue; ABABB (RPO, GALE, EBSCO); iambic tetrameter (EBSCO; GALE disagrees); one block; "mine, mine" possessiveness (EBSCO "possess Porphyria permanently"); blasphemous ending (EBSCO "lack of conscience").
- PMT lacks (found): Monthly Repository Jan 1836 as "Porphyria"; "Madhouse Cells" with Johannes Agricola (1842); 1849 title; hidden 12 five-line stanzas (RPO); Landow's unreliable-narrator points; AQA's "more cynical presentation of love" wording.

## Gaps and conflicts
- CONFLICT: metre — tetrameter (EBSCO, our count) vs pentameter (GALE). Use tetrameter.
- SINGLE SOURCE: January issue (GALE; ORR confirms Monthly Repository); 1863 (GALE); Fox as editor (ORR); 1889 coinage of the medical term (VW-W, student); purple/royalty (VW-W); Victorian "fallen" woman point (GALE); critical readings (EBSCO, Landow, Cho).
- GAP: no Browning statement on his purpose; no source linking the poem to marriage law.
