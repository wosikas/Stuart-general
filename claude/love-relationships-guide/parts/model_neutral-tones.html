MODEL ANSWER · Q25 · WRITTEN TO AQA'S TOP LEVEL (26–30 MARKS)

Neutral Tones and Winter Swans

“Compare how poets present powerful feelings about love in ‘Neutral Tones’ and in one other poem from ‘Love and relationships’.” [30 marks]
AQA, June 2024, Question 25
This answer was written to match AQA's Level 6 descriptors. It has not been marked by AQA, and no one can guarantee a mark, but every part is designed to hit a top-level requirement. About 700 words: a realistic length for 45 minutes.

Step 1: the 5-minute plan

  • Pick the partner: the question is about powerful feelings about love, so choose Winter Swans: another couple by cold water in winter, but a hopeful ending. The June 2024 mark scheme names this pairing: “possible comparison between bleak and more positive views of love in ‘Winter Swans’”.
  • Big idea (thesis): both poets show powerful feelings quietly, through silence and a wintry landscape. Hardy's moment of lost love hardens into lifelong bitterness; Sheers's silent quarrel turns into a lasting bond.
  • Comparison points: 1. Hardy's drained landscape; 2. the dying love, spoken to “you”; 3. Sheers's storm and the swans; 4. the endings: trapped by the pond against hands “settling after flight”. Then context and a judgement.

Step 2: the answer, with what earns the marks

Introduction Both Hardy and Sheers present powerful feelings about love through restraint: in each poem a couple stand by cold water in winter, and the landscape carries the feelings they cannot say. However, the poems move in opposite directions. In Neutral Tones, the moment love dies becomes a bitterness the speaker can never escape; in Winter Swans, a couple who begin in silence are drawn back together. Hardy suggests that the most powerful feeling love leaves behind is pain, while Sheers suggests it is a quiet, lasting bond, which makes Neutral Tones the more disturbing poem.
AO1
  • Answers the question in the first sentence, using its key words, “powerful feelings about love”.
  • Gives a concept that links both poems (feelings shown through restraint), then the key difference.
  • Ends with a judgement (“more disturbing”) to prove later.
Mark scheme (2025), Level 6: “It takes a conceptualised approach to the full task supported by a range of judicious references to the text.” Examiners (2020): students who began with “a thesis statement … tended to be more successful with AO1.”
Paragraph 1 · Hardy's drained landscape Hardy paints the end of love in drained colours. The “sun was white, as though chidden of God,”: “chidden” means scolded, so even the sun seems punished and has lost its warmth. The ground is “starving sod;”, and the fallen leaves “were grey.” This pathetic fallacy makes nature as lifeless as the relationship. The title fits: “neutral” tones are colourless, and perhaps the feelings here are so powerful that they have drained the world of colour, leaving only numbness.
AO1AO2
  • Short, precise quotations, built into sentences.
  • Zooms in on single words (“chidden”, “starving”) and explains what they suggest.
  • Uses the title as evidence, tentatively (“perhaps”).
Mark scheme (2024): “any valid comparison of use of natural imagery to present ideas/feelings, such as nature in ‘Winter Swans’ or ‘The Farmer’s Bride’”. Examiners (2023): “A well-selected word or phrase from the text is often much more effective than a long direct quotation.”
Paragraph 2 · love dying in front of him Hardy speaks directly to the woman, which makes the poem feel like an accusation. “Your eyes on me were as eyes that rove”: her gaze wanders, as if he were one of the “tedious riddles of years ago;”, an old puzzle she now finds boring. Their talk is a contest about which of them “lost the more by our love.” Love has become a loss. The paradox that her smile is “the deadest thing” yet “Alive enough to have strength to die;” shows love dying in front of him, and the simile “Like an ominous bird a-wing…” turns her bitter grin into an omen. The ellipsis leaves the threat hanging.
AO1AO2
  • Explains the effect of perspective (speaking to “you” sounds like blame).
  • Names a method (paradox, simile) only to explain what it shows about the feeling.
  • Notices punctuation (the ellipsis) because it creates an effect.
Mark scheme (2024): “any treatment of perspective to present ideas, such as direct address in ‘Neutral Tones’ compared with ‘Winter Swans’ or ‘Love’s Philosophy’”. Level 6: “a fine-grained and insightful analysis of methods”.
Paragraph 3 · Sheers: a storm, then the swans Sheers also opens with a bleak landscape after a storm: “The clouds had given their all”, as if they have argued themselves out, like the couple. The personified earth is “gulping for breath at our feet”, suggesting a relationship that is drowning. The couple walk “silent and apart,”: like Hardy's pair, they are together but emotionally distant. But where Hardy's bird is an omen, Sheers's swans are a lesson. They are “tipping in unison.”, the togetherness the couple lacks, and after they have “halved themselves in the dark water,” they rise again “like boats righting in rough weather.” The swans show that love can survive a storm.
AO1AO2
  • Links back to the first poem (cold setting; bird against swans), so the comparison runs through the essay.
  • Explains imagery by what it suggests about the relationship, not just its name.
  • Tracks feelings: distance, then hope.
Mark scheme (2024): “possible comparison between bleak and more positive views of love in ‘Winter Swans’ or ‘Singh Song!’ or ‘Sonnet 29 – I think of thee!’”.
Paragraph 4 · the endings: trapped or settled The endings differ most. Hardy's last stanza jumps forward: “Since then,” experience has taught him “keen lessons that love deceives,” and “wrings with wrong,”, where the harsh alliteration sounds like twisting pain. Yet these lessons only bring back the same scene, ending on “And a pond edged with greyish leaves.” The circular structure traps him in one memory: one powerful feeling has become a belief about all love. Sheers ends in movement instead. After “They mate for life”, the speaker admits “I didn't reply”, but their hands answer: they have “swum the distance between us” and end “like a pair of wings settling after flight.” The word “pair” echoes the swans, and “settling” suggests the conflict is over. Feeling is shown through touch, not words.
AO1AO2
  • Structure through the ending (circular against forward-moving), tied to meaning.
  • Explores a tiny word (“pair”, “settling”) to support a big idea.
  • No rhyme-scheme spotting, even though Neutral Tones rhymes ABBA.
Mark scheme (2024): “any valid treatment of different types of powerful feelings, eg joy in ‘Sonnet 29 – I think of thee!’ or hope for the future in ‘Winter Swans’”. Examiners (2023): helpful structure points are “openings/endings”. Examiners (2022): “There is a risk of comments on poetic construction becoming rather descriptive”.
Paragraph 5 · context linked to ideas Hardy dated the poem 1867, but it was not published until 1898, over thirty years later. He later “rejected the Victorian belief in a benevolent God”, and it is tempting to link this to the “God curst sun,”, though that is a link we make, not something the poem states. Hardy also warned that his poems were often “dramatic or personative”, so the speaker need not be Hardy himself. Sheers's poem comes from Skirrid Hill (2005), named after a hill whose Welsh name comes from a word meaning “divorce or separation”. In that collection, relationships are almost always broken “by death, disagreement, or some other parting”, which makes this couple's reunion stand out. Both poets explore what powerful feelings do to the self: one man is made bitter for life, while the other couple learn that love can last.

  • Context explains why the feelings differ (a cold universe; a book about separation), and links to the question.
  • Careful, not over-claimed: the link to Hardy's beliefs is labelled as a reading, and the speaker is not assumed to be Hardy.

Conclusion · the judgement Both poets present powerful feelings about love quietly, through cold water, silence and nature. But Hardy shows the death of love as a single moment that poisons every later feeling, while Sheers shows a near-ending that turns into reconciliation. Neutral Tones is the more disturbing poem because its feelings never move on: the speaker is still standing by the pond, while Sheers's couple walk on into the afternoon light, together.

  • Makes a clear evaluative judgement and gives a reason for it.
  • Short: the conclusion pulls ideas together and adds no new quotations.

Checklist: why this reaches the top level

  • ✔ One big idea (a thesis) stated at the start and proved all the way through.
  • ✔ Feelings named throughout (bitterness, boredom, distance, hope, tenderness), because the question asks about powerful feelings.
  • ✔ Short quotations, with individual words explained.
  • ✔ Every technique linked to its effect and to love.
  • ✔ Structure through openings and endings, not rhyme schemes.
  • ✔ Context used to explain ideas, briefly and carefully, never as a history lesson.
  • ✔ A clear judgement at the end.
